\emph{Bi-log-concave laws.} $\B$ is the class of laws that are point masses or have a continuous distribution function $F$ with $\log F$ and $\log(1 - F)$ concave \citep*{dumbgen2017}; $\LC \subset \B$. Both classes are closed under translation and under multiplication by a nonzero constant: a negative constant exchanges the roles of $\log F$ and $\log(1 - F)$. The following facts are used throughout.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A law in $\B$ that is not a point mass has no atom and has a density $f$ that is positive on the interior of its support, the hazard $f/(1 - F)$ is nondecreasing and $f/F$ is nonincreasing, and $f' \le f^2/F$ where $f$ is differentiable \citep*{dumbgen2017}.
\item $\B$ is closed under convolution with a log-concave law. If $\log F_X$ is concave and $g$ is a log-concave density, $(t, y) \mapsto F_X(t - y)g(y)$ is log-concave on $\mathbb R^2$, so $F_{X+Y}(t) = \int F_X(t - y)g(y)\,dy$ is log-concave by the theorem of \citet{prekopa1973}, and likewise $1 - F_{X+Y}$.
\item $\B$ is closed under weak convergence \citep{saumard2019}: concavity of $\log F_n$ and $\log(1 - F_n)$ passes to the limit at continuity points, and a limit that is not a point mass has no atom, because a concave function is continuous on the interior of its domain.
\item If $X$ has a continuous distribution function $F$ with $\log F$ concave and a finite mean, then $F\{E(X)\} \ge \mathrm{e}^{-1}$: $F(X)$ is uniform, so $E\{\log F(X)\} = -1$, and Jensen's inequality gives $\log F\{E(X)\} \ge E\{\log F(X)\}$. If also $\log(1 - F)$ is concave, $1 - F\{E(X)\} \ge \mathrm{e}^{-1}$ in the same way.
\item For the exponential tail $b - E_u$, the distribution function is $\min\{1, \mathrm{e}^{u(y - b)}\}$; it is log-concave, and if $\log F_Y$ is concave and $u$ is a supergradient of $\log F_Y$ at a point $v$ with $F_Y(v) = q$, then $F_Y(y) \le \min\{1, q\mathrm{e}^{u(y - v)}\}$ for all $y$, that is, $Y$ is stochastically larger than $v + u^{-1}\log(1/q) - E_u$.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{A bimodal law in $\B$.} The bimodal latent law of \S~\ref{M-sec:cost} is the mixture $\pi N(m_1, s^2) + (1 - \pi)N(m_2, s^2)$ with mean zero, variance one, $\pi = 0.4$ and $(m_2 - m_1)/s = 2.5$, that is $m_1 = -0.94868$, $m_2 = 0.63246$ and $s = 0.63246$. It is bimodal, since bimodality starts at a separation of about $2.443$ for $\pi = 0.4$ (numerical), and it lies in $\B$ by the following lemma and the certificate after it; with $\pi = 0.4$ the mixture stays in $\B$ up to a separation of about $2.59$ (numerical).

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bimodal}
Let $f$ be the density of $\pi N(m_1, s^2) + (1 - \pi)N(m_2, s^2)$ with $m_1 < m_2$ and $r = (m_2 - m_1)/s > 2$, let $P(w)$ be the posterior probability of the first component, and let $a < b$ solve $P(a) = \tfrac12 + h$ and $P(b) = \tfrac12 - h$, with $h = (1/4 - 1/r^2)^{1/2}$. The mixture is in $\B$ if $f'F < f^2$ on $[a, m_2]$ and $-f'(1 - F) < f^2$ on $[m_1, b]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $(\log f)'' = -s^{-2} + P(1 - P)r^2s^{-2}$ and $P$ decreases, $f$ is log-concave on $(-\infty, a]$ and on $[b, \infty)$. On $(-\infty, a]$, $F(t) = \int 1(y \le t)f(y)1(y \le a)\,dy$ is the marginal of a log-concave function of $(t, y)$, so $\log F$ is concave there \citep{prekopa1973}; likewise $\log(1 - F)$ on $[b, \infty)$. Where $f' \le 0$, in particular on $[m_2, \infty)$, $f'F \le f^2$ is trivial, and where $f' \ge 0$, in particular on $(-\infty, m_1]$, so is $-f'(1 - F) \le f^2$. With the two hypotheses, $(\log F)'' = (f'F - f^2)/F^2 \le 0$ and $\{\log(1 - F)\}'' = \{-f'(1 - F) - f^2\}/(1 - F)^2 \le 0$ on the whole line.
\end{proof}

For the law above, the two hypotheses are proved in Arb ball arithmetic on $128$ boxes, with the ends $a$ and $b$ rounded outward (\S~\ref{sec:s-ball}); the largest values of $(f'F - f^2)/f^2$ on $[a, m_2]$ and of $\{-f'(1 - F) - f^2\}/f^2$ on $[m_1, b]$ are $-0.141$ and $-0.864$.

\section{Coverage transfer}\label{sec:s-transfer}

This section proves the results of \S~\ref{M-sec:transfer}.

\subsection{Both ends of the interval}\label{sec:s-twoend}

Let $\lambda_{\E}(p') = \sup\{q' \in (0, 1) : \Psi_{\E}(q') < p'\}$, with $\sup\emptyset = 0$, and $\vartheta_{\E}(a) = 1 - \lambda_{\E}(1 - a)$; both are nondecreasing. Let $\beta_{\E} = \inf_{\epsilon \in \E}\pr(\epsilon \ge 0)$. Since $\min(1, q'\mathrm{e}^{-\epsilon}) \le q'$ when $\epsilon \ge 0$, $\Psi_{\E}(q') \le 1 - (1 - q')\beta_{\E}$. If $\beta_{\E} > 0$, as for Gaussian and symmetric unimodal noise, where $\beta_{\E} = 1/2$, and for mean-zero log-concave noise, where $\beta_{\E} = \mathrm{e}^{-1}$ by fact 4 of \S~\ref{sec:s-prelim}, then $\Psi_{\E}(q') < 1$ for every $q' < 1$, so $\lambda_{\E}(1) = 1$ and $\vartheta_{\E}(0) = 0$. If instead $\Psi_{\E}(q') = 1$ for some $q' < 1$, Proposition~\ref{M-prop:general} shows that no noisy level below $1$ gives latent coverage $q'$ at one end, so no transfer is possible; this is the case of mean-zero unimodal noise, Theorem~\ref{M-thm:classes}(iii).

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:twoend}
Let $\beta_{\E} > 0$, $W \in \B$, $t > 0$ and $e = \sigma\epsilon$ with $\sigma > 0$ and the law of $\epsilon$ in $\E$, and let $a_R = \pr(W + e > t)$ and $a_L = \pr(W + e < -t)$. Then $\pr(W > t) \le \vartheta_{\E}(a_R)$ and $\pr(W < -t) \le \vartheta_{\E}(a_L)$. Consequently, if $\pr(|W + e| \le t) \ge p > p_{\E}(q)$, then $\pr(|W| \le t) \ge q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The law of $Y = (W - t)/\sigma$ is in $\B$, so $\log F_Y$ is concave, and $\pr(Y + \epsilon \le 0) = 1 - a_R$. For every $q'$ with $\Psi_{\E}(q') < 1 - a_R$, Proposition~\ref{M-prop:general} gives $F_Y(0) \ge q'$, so $\pr(W \le t) \ge \lambda_{\E}(1 - a_R)$, which is the first bound. The law of $Y' = (-W - t)/\sigma$ is in $\B$ too, and $-\epsilon$ has a law in $\E$, which is closed under negation; as $Y' - \epsilon \le 0$ exactly when $W + e \ge -t$, $\pr(Y' - \epsilon \le 0) = 1 - a_L$, and the same argument gives $\pr(W \ge -t) = F_{Y'}(0) \ge \lambda_{\E}(1 - a_L)$, which is the second bound. Finally, the property in the definition~(\ref{M-eq:pE}) of $p_{\E}(q)$ is monotone in $p$, so it holds at every $p > p_{\E}(q)$; as $a_L + a_R = 1 - \pr(|W + e| \le t) \le 1 - p$, it gives $\pr(|W| > t) \le \vartheta_{\E}(a_L) + \vartheta_{\E}(a_R) \le 1 - q$.
\end{proof}

In the proof of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank} Lemma~\ref{lem:twoend} is applied to one unit at a time, so the noise laws and scales may differ between units. Since $\vartheta_{\E}$ is nondecreasing, the condition in~(\ref{M-eq:pE}) can be checked on finitely many splits $(a_L, a_R)$, as in \S~\ref{sec:s-ball}; a certificate replaces $\lambda_{\E}$ by a smaller certified level, which only enlarges $\vartheta_{\E}$ and so keeps the bound valid.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:convex}
Suppose that $\Psi_{\E} = 1/2$ on $(0, q_0]$ and that $\Psi_{\E}$ is continuous, strictly increasing and convex on $[q_0, 1)$, with $\Psi_{\E}(q) \to 1$ as $q \to 1$. Then $p_{\E}(q) \le \Psi_{\E}(q)$ for every $q \in (q_0, 1)$, and noisy coverage $p \in (1/2, 1)$ at threshold $t$ guarantees latent coverage $\lambda_{\E}(p) = \Psi_{\E}^{-1}(p)$ at $t$ for every $W \in \B$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On $(1/2, 1)$, $\lambda_{\E}$ is the inverse of the increasing convex function $\Psi_{\E}$ restricted to $(q_0, 1)$, so it is increasing and concave, and $\lambda_{\E}(1) = 1$. Hence $\vartheta_{\E}(a) = 1 - \lambda_{\E}(1 - a)$ is convex on $[0, 1/2)$ with $\vartheta_{\E}(0) = 0$, and therefore superadditive there: for $a, b \ge 0$ with $a + b < 1/2$, convexity gives $\vartheta_{\E}(a) \le \{a/(a + b)\}\vartheta_{\E}(a + b)$ and the same for $b$. With $a_L + a_R \le 1 - p < 1/2$, Lemma~\ref{lem:twoend} and monotonicity give $\pr(|W| > t) \le \vartheta_{\E}(a_L) + \vartheta_{\E}(a_R) \le \vartheta_{\E}(1 - p) = 1 - \lambda_{\E}(p)$. If $p > \Psi_{\E}(q) > 1/2$, then $\lambda_{\E}(p) > q$, so $p_{\E}(q) \le \Psi_{\E}(q)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{One end is not enough}\label{sec:s-oneend}

No noisy level below $\Psi_{\E}(q)$ suffices, even for log-concave $W$; with Lemma~\ref{lem:convex} this gives $p_{\E}(q) = \Psi_{\E}(q)$ whenever $\Psi_{\E}$ is convex. Suppose that $p < \Psi_{\E}(q)$, take $\epsilon$ with law in $\E$ and $E\{\min(1, q\mathrm{e}^{-\epsilon})\} > p$, let $a = \log(1/q)$, and take $\eta > 0$ so small that $E\{\min(1, q\mathrm{e}^{-\eta - \epsilon})\} > p$, which is possible by dominated convergence. Let $Y = a + \eta - E_1$, so that $F_Y(0) = q\mathrm{e}^{-\eta} < q$ and $\pr(Y + \epsilon \le 0) = E\{\min(1, q\mathrm{e}^{-\eta - \epsilon})\}$, and for $s > 0$ let $W = t + sY$, which is log-concave, with noise $s\epsilon$. Then $\pr(|W| \le t) \le \pr(W \le t) = q\mathrm{e}^{-\eta} < q$, while
\[
\pr(|W + s\epsilon| \le t) = \pr(Y + \epsilon \le 0) - \pr(Y + \epsilon < -2t/s) \to E\{\min(1, q\mathrm{e}^{-\eta - \epsilon})\},
\]
which exceeds $p$,
as $s \to 0$. So noisy coverage $p$ does not guarantee latent coverage $q$. The construction has only one end of the interval in play: the left end is $2t/s$ tail scales away.

\subsection{Symmetric unimodal noise}\label{sec:s-su}

By the theorem of Khintchine \citep[Chapter~1]{dharmadhikari1988}, a law symmetric and unimodal about $0$ is that of $RU$, with $U$ uniform on $[-1, 1]$ and $R \ge 0$ independent of $U$. Hence $E\{\min(1, q\mathrm{e}^{-RU})\} = E\{g(R)\} \le \sup_r g(r)$, with $g(r) = E\{\min(1, q\mathrm{e}^{-rU})\}$, and uniform laws attain the supremum. With $\ell = \log q < 0$,
\[
g(r) = q\,\frac{\sinh r}{r}\ (r \le -\ell), \qquad g(r) = \frac{\ell + r + 1 - q\mathrm{e}^{-r}}{2r}\ (r \ge -\ell).
\]
The first branch increases. On the second, $g'(r)$ has the sign of $d(r) = q\mathrm{e}^{-r}(1 + r) - (1 + \ell)$, which decreases, since $d'(r) = -qr\mathrm{e}^{-r}$. If $q \le \mathrm{e}^{-1}$, then $1 + \ell \le 0 < d(r)$, so $g$ increases throughout and $\Psi_{\E}(q) = \lim_{r\to\infty} g(r) = 1/2$, not attained. If $q > \mathrm{e}^{-1}$, then $d(-\ell) = \mathrm{e}^{2\ell}(1 - \ell) - 1 - \ell > 0$, because this function of $\ell$ vanishes with its derivative at $0$ and has second derivative $-4\ell\mathrm{e}^{2\ell} > 0$ for $\ell < 0$, and $d(r) \to -(1 + \ell) < 0$; so $g$ is maximal at the root $r$ of $d$, where $g(r) = (1 + q\mathrm{e}^{-r})/2$. This proves the formula of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:classes}(i) and shows that, as for Gaussian noise, $\Psi_{\E}(q) = 1/2$ for $q \le \mathrm{e}^{-1}$. Heavy tails are allowed: the $t_3$ and Cauchy laws are symmetric and unimodal.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:su-exact}
For symmetric unimodal noise, $\Psi = \Psi_{\E}$ is increasing and strictly convex on $(\mathrm{e}^{-1}, 1)$, with $\Psi(q) \to 1/2$ as $q \downarrow \mathrm{e}^{-1}$ and $\Psi(q) \to 1$ as $q \to 1$. Hence $p_{\E}(q) = \Psi(q)$ for $\mathrm{e}^{-1} < q < 1$, and noisy coverage $p \in (1/2, 1)$ guarantees latent coverage $\lambda_{\E}(p) = \Psi^{-1}(p)$ for every $W \in \B$ and every symmetric unimodal noise, possibly different between units; no larger latent level is guaranteed, even for log-concave $W$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $s = q\mathrm{e}^{-r}$ with $r$ the root of $d$, so that $\Psi(q) = (1 + s)/2$ and $s(1 + r) = 1 + \ell$. Since $\partial d/\partial r < 0$, $r$ is differentiable in $q$ by the implicit function theorem; differentiating $s(1 + r) = 1 + \ell$ with $ds = s(dq/q - dr)$ gives $rs\,dr/dq = \ell/q$ and, using $\ell = s(1 + r) - 1$, $ds/dq = (rs - \ell)/(qr) = (1 - s)/(qr)$. Hence $\Psi'(q) = (1 - s)/(2qr) > 0$ and, after the same substitution,
\[
\Psi''(q) = \frac{(1 - s)\{1 - s(1 + r)^2\}}{2q^2r^3s}, \qquad 1 - s(1 + r)^2 = 1 - (1 + \ell)(1 + r).
\]
Let $x = 1 + \ell \in (0, 1)$ and $R = 1/x - 1$. As $d$ decreases on $(0, \infty)$, $r < R$ if and only if $d(R) < 0$, that is $\ell - R < 2\log x$, that is $k(x) = 2\log x - x + 1/x > 0$; and $k(1) = 0$, $k'(x) = -(1 - x)^2/x^2 < 0$. So $(1 + \ell)(1 + r) < 1$ and $\Psi'' > 0$. As $q \to 1$, $0 < r < R \to 0$ and $s \to 1$, so $\Psi(q) \to 1$; as $q \downarrow \mathrm{e}^{-1}$, $x \to 0$, the root $r$ tends to infinity and $s = (1 + \ell)/(1 + r) \to 0$, so $\Psi(q) \to 1/2$. Lemma~\ref{lem:convex} with $q_0 = \mathrm{e}^{-1}$ gives the guarantee and $p_{\E}(q) \le \Psi(q)$; \S~\ref{sec:s-oneend}, with $\epsilon$ uniform on $[-r, r]$, gives the converse, and the same construction with $q' > \lambda_{\E}(p)$, for which $\Psi(q') > p$, shows that $\lambda_{\E}(p)$ cannot be raised.
\end{proof}

At $q = 0.8$, $0.9$ and $0.95$ the maximizing uniform half-widths are $r = 0.26226$, $0.11332$ and $0.05311$, and the levels are $\Psi(q) = 0.8077245$, $0.9017873$ and $0.9504311$ (ball arithmetic, enclosures of width below $10^{-15}$); noisy coverage $0.9$ guarantees exactly latent coverage $\lambda_{\E}(0.9) = 0.8981432$. A certificate of the splits of \S~\ref{sec:s-ball}, run for this class as an independent check, gives $p_{\E} \le 0.8078$, $0.9018$ and $0.9505$ at $q = 0.8$, $0.9$ and $0.95$, in agreement with Proposition~\ref{prop:su-exact}.

\subsection{Mean-zero log-concave noise}\label{sec:s-lc}

\emph{Reduction.} Let $h(y) = \min(1, q\mathrm{e}^{-y})$, which is continuous, nonincreasing and $1$-Lipschitz. If $\epsilon$ is log-concave with mean zero, conditioning on $|\epsilon| \le M$ and recentring changes $E\{h(\epsilon)\}$ by at most $2\pr(|\epsilon| > M) + |E(\epsilon \mid |\epsilon| \le M)|$, which tends to zero; so it suffices to bound $E\{h(\epsilon)\}$ over log-concave laws on compact intervals with $E(\epsilon) \ge 0$, a larger class. By the localization theorem \citep{fradelizi2004}, the supremum of this linear functional over that class is attained at a point mass or at a law with density proportional to $\mathrm{e}^{\beta y}$ on a segment. A law with $E(\epsilon) > 0$ can be translated to mean zero without decreasing $E\{h(\epsilon)\}$, because $h$ is nonincreasing. So the supremum is over the point mass at $0$, where $h(0) = q$, and the mean-zero log-affine laws on segments. Write the latter as $\epsilon = L(V - \mu_k)$, with $V$ on $[0, 1]$ with density proportional to $\mathrm{e}^{kv}$, $k = \beta L$ and $\mu_k = E(V)$; then, with $\ell = \log q$ and $Z_k = \int_0^1\mathrm{e}^{kv}\,dv$,
\[
E\{h(\epsilon)\} = \frac{1}{Z_k}\Big\{\int_0^{v_c}\mathrm{e}^{kv}\,dv + q\mathrm{e}^{L\mu_k}\int_{v_c}^1\mathrm{e}^{(k - L)v}\,dv\Big\},
\]
with $v_c = \min\{1, \max(0, \mu_k + \ell/L)\}$.
For $|k| \ge 2$ we use the scale $\sigma = L/|k|$ and the truncated exponential form $\epsilon = \pm\sigma(\tau - m)$, with $\tau$ standard exponential truncated to $[0, |k|]$ and $m = E(\tau)$, which includes $|k| = \infty$, the centred exponential laws $\pm\sigma(E_1 - 1)$. As $|k| \ge 2$, $m$ lies between $m_2 = 1 - 2/(\mathrm{e}^2 - 1)$ and $1$.

\emph{Bounds.} A branch and bound in ball arithmetic bounds $E\{h(\epsilon)\}$ from above on boxes of $(k, L)$ for $|k| \le 2$ and of $(1/|k|, \sigma)$ for $|k| \ge 2$, splitting until each box is below the target. On a box the bound is $E\{h(\epsilon)\} \le \pr(\epsilon \le u) + qE\{\mathrm{e}^{-\epsilon}; \epsilon > u\}$, which holds for every cut $u$ because $h(y) \le 1(y \le u) + q\mathrm{e}^{-y}1(y > u)$; the cut is fixed at the kink of $h$ for the centre of the box, so that the bound is tight to second order. The ends of the scale parameter are closed analytically.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For small $L$, $E\{h(\epsilon)\} \le qE(\mathrm{e}^{-\epsilon}) \le q\mathrm{e}^{L^2/24}$: with $K(s) = \log\int_0^1\mathrm{e}^{sv}\,dv$, $\log E(\mathrm{e}^{-\epsilon}) = K(k - L) - K(k) + LK'(k) = \int_{k - L}^{k}(s - k + L)K''(s)\,ds \le L^2/24$, because $K''(s) = s^{-2} - \{2\sinh(s/2)\}^{-2}$, the variance of the law on $[0, 1]$ with density proportional to $\mathrm{e}^{sv}$, is at most its value $1/12$ at $s = 0$.
\item For large $L$, $E\{h(\epsilon)\} \le \pr(\epsilon \le 4) + q\mathrm{e}^{-4} \le 1 - \mathrm{e}^{-1} + 4d/L + q\mathrm{e}^{-4}$, by fact 4 of \S~\ref{sec:s-prelim} applied to $V$ and because the density of $V$ is at most $d = 2/(1 - \mathrm{e}^{-2})$ for $|k| \le 2$; this is below the target once $L$ exceeds a threshold found in ball arithmetic.
\item For the truncated exponential form, with $\pr(\tau \le y) \le (1 - \mathrm{e}^{-y})/(1 - \mathrm{e}^{-2})$ and $\pr(\tau \ge y) \le \mathrm{e}^{-y}/(1 - \mathrm{e}^{-2})$: $E\{h(\epsilon)\} \le q\mathrm{e}^{\sigma}$ for $\epsilon = \sigma(\tau - m)$ and $E\{h(\epsilon)\} \le q\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma m_2}/(1 - \sigma)$ for $\epsilon = \sigma(m - \tau)$ and $\sigma < 1$, at the small end, and $E\{h(\epsilon)\} \le \pr(\tau \le 1 + 4/\sigma) + q\mathrm{e}^{-4}$ and $\pr(\tau \ge m_2 - 4/\sigma) + q\mathrm{e}^{-4}$ at the large end.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Results.} At $q = 0.9$ the certificate gives $\Psi_{\E}(0.9) < 0.9052$, and the centred exponential law $\sigma(E_1 - 1)$ with $\sigma = 0.11777$ gives $\Psi_{\E}(0.9) > 0.90517$; the value at that law is $0.9051755$. For the two-sided level, a partition of the noisy failure probability $a_L \in [0, (1 - p)/2]$ at the left end is proposed from the values at the centred exponential law; on each interval $[a_0, a_1]$ the bound $\vartheta_{\E}(a_1) + \vartheta_{\E}(1 - p - a_0)$ is evaluated at certified lower bounds of $\lambda_{\E}$, obtained by the same branch and bound. This certifies the noisy level $0.906$ for latent level $0.9$, with $41$ intervals, and noisy level $0.9$ for latent level $0.8935$, with $45$ intervals. Numerically the worst split puts the whole failure probability at one end, as for Gaussian and symmetric unimodal noise, so the two-sided level would be $\Psi_{\E}(0.9) = 0.905176$; this is not used.

\emph{An open case.} That the centred exponential law attains $\Psi_{\E}$ is supported by the certificate but not proved. Laws bounded above by $c = -\ell$ are covered analytically: if $X = -\epsilon \le s$ almost surely with $0 < s \le c$, the log-density of $X$ minus the affine log-density of $s(1 - E_1)$ is concave on $(-\infty, s]$, so the difference of densities changes sign at most twice, and with equal means $X$ is smaller than $s(1 - E_1)$ in the convex order; as $\Psi_{\E}(q) = q\sup E\{\mathrm{e}^{\min(X, c)}\}$ and $x \mapsto \mathrm{e}^{\min(x, c)}$ is convex on $(-\infty, s]$, such laws do no better than $s(1 - E_1)$. The extremal law has mass above $c$: at $q = 0.9$, $\sigma = 0.11777 > c = 0.10536$.

\subsection{Mean-zero unimodal noise}\label{sec:s-mzu}

Let $\epsilon$ have density $(1 - \rho)/a$ on $[-a, 0]$ and $\rho/b$ on $(0, b]$, with $b = (1 - \rho)a/\rho$ and $0 < \rho \le 1/2$; it is unimodal with mean zero. Since $h(y) = 1$ for $y \le \ell = \log q$, $E\{h(\epsilon)\} \ge \pr(\epsilon \le \ell) = (1 - \rho)(1 + \ell/a)$, which tends to one as $\rho \to 0$ and $a \to \infty$. So $\Psi_{\E}(q) = 1$, and no noisy level below one transfers to any latent level $q \in (0, 1)$.

\subsection{Gaussian noise: proof of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:gauss}}\label{sec:s-gauss-exact}

Let $a = \log(1/q)$, $m(b) = \phi(b)/\Phi(b)$ and $A(b) = m(b)\{b + m(b)\}$. For $Z$ standard normal, $E(Z \mid Z \le b) = -m(b)$ and $E(Z^2 \mid Z \le b) = 1 - bm(b)$, so $A(b) = 1 - \var(Z \mid Z \le b)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sampford}
(a) $m'(b) = -A(b)$. (b) $0 < A(b) < 1$ for every real $b$, $A(b) \to 1$ as $b \to -\infty$ and $A(b) \to 0$ as $b \to \infty$. (c) $A$ is strictly decreasing.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) is direct differentiation. In (b), $A < 1$ because a truncated normal law has positive variance, and $A > 0$ because $m(b) > 0$ and $b + m(b) = \Phi(b)^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^b\Phi(z)\,dz > 0$; the last identity holds since both $\phi(b) + b\Phi(b)$ and $\int_{-\infty}^b\Phi$ have derivative $\Phi(b)$ and vanish at $-\infty$. As $b \to \infty$, $m(b) \to 0$ and $bm(b) \to 0$; as $b \to -\infty$, $m(b) = |b| + |b|^{-1} + O(|b|^{-3})$, so $A(b) \to 1$. Part (c) is inequality~(4) of \citet{sampford1953}: in his notation $\nu(x) = \phi(x)/\{1 - \Phi(x)\}$ and $\lambda(x) = \nu(x)\{\nu(x) - x\}$, so that $m(b) = \nu(-b)$ and $A(b) = \lambda(-b)$, and he proves that $\lambda$ is strictly increasing on the real line.
\end{proof}

\emph{The derivative in $s$.} Let $b = a/s - s$. Since $(a/s)^2 - (a/s - s)^2 = 2a - s^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identity}
q\mathrm{e}^{s^2/2}\phi(a/s - s) = \phi(a/s).
\end{equation}
Differentiating $\Gamma(q, s) = \Phi(-a/s) + q\mathrm{e}^{s^2/2}\Phi(b)$, with $db/ds = -a/s^2 - 1$, and using~(\ref{eq:identity}),
\begin{align*}
\partial_s\Gamma &= \phi(a/s)\frac{a}{s^2} + q\mathrm{e}^{s^2/2}\Big\{s\Phi(b) - \phi(b)\Big(\frac{a}{s^2} + 1\Big)\Big\} \\
&= qs\mathrm{e}^{s^2/2}\Phi(b) - \phi(a/s) = \phi(a/s)\Big\{\frac{s}{m(b)} - 1\Big\},
\end{align*}
since $q\mathrm{e}^{s^2/2}\Phi(b) = q\mathrm{e}^{s^2/2}\phi(b)/m(b) = \phi(a/s)/m(b)$. So $\partial_s\Gamma$ has the sign of $s - m(b)$.

\emph{The sign change.} For fixed $a > 0$, $s \mapsto b = a/s - s$ is a strictly decreasing bijection of $(0, \infty)$ onto $\mathbb R$, and $s^2 + bs = a$. The map $t \mapsto t^2 + bt$ is strictly increasing on $t > -b/2$, and both $s$ and $m(b)$ lie there: $s = \{-b + (b^2 + 4a)^{1/2}\}/2 > -b/2$, and $m(b) > \max(0, -b) \ge -b/2$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:sampford}(b). Hence
\[
s > m(b) \iff a = s^2 + bs > m(b)^2 + bm(b) = A(b).
\]
As $s$ increases, $b$ decreases, and $A(b)$ increases strictly through all of $(0, 1)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:sampford}.

\emph{Part (i).} If $a \ge 1$, that is $q \le \mathrm{e}^{-1}$, then $a > A(b)$ for every $s$, so $\Gamma(q, \cdot)$ is strictly increasing. As $s \to \infty$, $\Phi(-a/s) \to 1/2$ and $q\mathrm{e}^{s^2/2}\Phi(b) = \phi(a/s)/m(b) \to 0$, because $b \to -\infty$ and $m(b) \to \infty$. So $\Psi_{\N}(q) = 1/2$, approached as $s \to \infty$ and not attained.

\emph{Part (ii).} If $a < 1$, $\partial_s\Gamma$ is positive before and negative after the unique $s^*$ with $A(b^*) = a$, so $s^*$ is the unique maximizer, and $s^* = m(b^*)$ there. At $s^*$, $a/s^* = b^* + s^* = c$, and $q\mathrm{e}^{s^{*2}/2}\Phi(b^*) = \phi(c)/m(b^*)$, which gives the closed form.

\emph{Part (iii): the derivative in $q$.} With $s$ fixed, by~(\ref{eq:identity}),
\[
\partial_aG = -\frac{\phi(a/s)}{s} - q\mathrm{e}^{s^2/2}\Phi(b) + q\mathrm{e}^{s^2/2}\frac{\phi(b)}{s} = -q\mathrm{e}^{s^2/2}\Phi(b),
\]
so $\partial_q\Gamma = -q^{-1}\partial_aG = \mathrm{e}^{s^2/2}\Phi(b)$. The maximizer $s^*(q) = m\{A^{-1}(\log 1/q)\}$ is continuous in $q$, as $A$ is a continuous strictly monotone bijection of $\mathbb R$ onto $(0, 1)$. For $q_1 < q_2$ in $(\mathrm{e}^{-1}, 1)$, with $s_j = s^*(q_j)$,
\[
\Gamma(q_2, s_1) - \Gamma(q_1, s_1) \le \Psi_{\N}(q_2) - \Psi_{\N}(q_1) \le \Gamma(q_2, s_2) - \Gamma(q_1, s_2);
\]
dividing by $q_2 - q_1$ and using the continuity of $s^*$ and of $\partial_q\Gamma$ in both arguments gives $\Psi_{\N}'(q) = \partial_q\Gamma\{q, s^*(q)\} = D(b^*)$ with $D(b) = \mathrm{e}^{m(b)^2/2}\Phi(b)$, which is continuous in $q$.

\emph{Monotonicity.} Along the maximizers, $q(b) = \mathrm{e}^{-A(b)}$ increases strictly from $\mathrm{e}^{-1}$ to $1$ as $b$ runs over $\mathbb R$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:sampford}. By Lemma~\ref{lem:sampford}(a),
\[
\frac{d}{db}\log D(b) = m(b)m'(b) + m(b) = m(b)\{1 - A(b)\} = m(b)\var(Z \mid Z \le b) > 0,
\]
so $\Psi_{\N}'$ is strictly increasing on $(\mathrm{e}^{-1}, 1)$ and $\Psi_{\N}$ is strictly convex there. As $q \downarrow \mathrm{e}^{-1}$, $b^* \to -\infty$; then $c = b^* + m(b^*) \to 0$, so $\Psi_{\N}(q) \to 1/2$, and $m^2 = b^2 + 2 + o(1)$ and $\Phi(b) = \phi(b)\{1 + o(1)\}/|b|$, so $D(b) = \mathrm{e}\{1 + o(1)\}/\{(2\pi)^{1/2}|b|\} \to 0$, the derivative of the constant part. As $\Psi_{\N}$ is continuous at $\mathrm{e}^{-1}$, the mean value theorem then gives a right derivative $0$ there, matching the left one. Hence $\Psi_{\N}$ is continuously differentiable and convex on $(0, 1)$. As $q \to 1$, $b^* \to \infty$, $c \to \infty$ and $\Psi_{\N}(q) \to 1$.

\emph{The two-sided level.} Lemma~\ref{lem:convex} with $q_0 = \mathrm{e}^{-1}$ gives $p_{\N}(q) \le \Psi_{\N}(q)$, and \S~\ref{sec:s-oneend} gives $p_{\N}(q) \ge \Psi_{\N}(q)$. This completes the proof. $\square$

The proof uses only Lemma~\ref{lem:sampford}. What it adds is the reduction: the first-order condition is $s = m(b)$, a Mills ratio of the noise; the envelope derivative is a tilted distribution function, $\mathrm{e}^{s^2/2}\Phi(b)$; and its monotonicity along the maximizers is the monotonicity of a truncated variance. It also explains the threshold: $\mathrm{e}^{-1} = \mathrm{e}^{-\sup A}$. Table~\ref{tab:s-gauss} gives the constants at three levels; the enclosures of $\Psi_{\N}(q)$ have width below $3 \times 10^{-16}$ (\S~\ref{sec:s-ball}).

\subsection{Unimodal latent laws: proof of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:latentshape}}\label{sec:s-latentshape}

The bound needs no shape. Given $W > 1$, the event $\epsilon \ge 0$ gives $W + \epsilon > 1$, and given $W < -1$ the event $\epsilon \le 0$ gives $W + \epsilon < -1$; both have probability at least $\beta_{\E}$, since $\E$ is closed under negation and $\epsilon$ is independent of $W$, so $1 - p \ge \pr(|W + \epsilon| > 1) \ge \beta_{\E}\pr(|W| > 1)$. For median-zero noise this is the bound of \citet{guille2024}; for mean-zero log-concave noise $\pr(\epsilon \ge 0) \ge \mathrm{e}^{-1}$ and $\pr(\epsilon \le 0) \ge \mathrm{e}^{-1}$ by fact 4 of \S~\ref{sec:s-prelim}, with equality for the centred exponential law $E_1 - 1$; for mean-zero unimodal noise $\beta_{\E} = 0$, by the laws of \S~\ref{sec:s-mzu}, for which $\pr(\epsilon \ge 0) = \rho$.

For the construction, let $\epsilon$ have a law in $\E$, continuous at $0$, with $\pr(\epsilon \ge 0) = \beta < \beta_{\E} + \gamma'$; as $p > 1 - \beta_{\E}$, $(1 - p)/\beta < 1$ for small $\gamma'$. Let $\tau, \gamma'' > 0$ be small and $0 < \omega < 1$, with $\omega$ to tend to $0$ with the noise scale. Let
\[
s = (1 - p - \gamma'' - \tau)/\beta, \qquad m = 1 - s - \tau, \qquad A = 2s(1 + \omega/2)/\tau - 1,
\]
so that $s, m > 0$, and let $W$ have a density made of three pieces: $m/2$ on $[-1, 1]$, the mass inside the interval; $s/\omega$ on $(1, 1 + \omega]$, a thin layer just outside the right end; and $\tau/(A - 1)$ on $[-A, -1)$, a long light left tail that makes $E(W) = s(1 + \omega/2) - \tau(A + 1)/2 = 0$. For small $\tau$ and $\omega$ the density is nondecreasing up to $1 + \omega$ and zero beyond it, so $W$ is unimodal. With noise $\sigma\epsilon$, the failure probability of $|W + \sigma\epsilon| \le 1$ splits over the three pieces: the inside piece contributes $(m/2)\int_{-1}^1\pr(|w + \sigma\epsilon| > 1)\,dw$, which tends to $0$ as $\sigma \to 0$ by dominated convergence; the layer contributes at most $s\{\pr(\epsilon > -\omega/\sigma) + \pr(\epsilon < -2/\sigma)\}$, where the second term tends to $0$ and the first tends to $\beta$ as $\sigma \to 0$ with $\omega/\sigma \to 0$, for instance $\omega = \sigma^2$, by continuity at $0$; and the left tail contributes at most $\tau$. So the noisy coverage tends to at least $1 - \tau - s\beta = p + \gamma'' > p$, while the latent coverage is $m = 1 - (1 - p - \gamma'' - \tau)/\beta - \tau$, within any $\gamma$ of $1 - (1 - p)/\beta_{\E}$ for small $\gamma'$, $\gamma''$ and $\tau$. If $\beta_{\E} = 0$, take $\beta < \gamma'$ and $s = 1 - \tau - \gamma$, so that the latent coverage is $m = \gamma$ while the noisy coverage tends to at least $1 - \tau - s\beta > p$ for small $\tau$ and $\gamma'$.

The noisy coverage of this law has a closed form: for a piece with uniform density on $[a, b]$, $\int_a^b F(c - w)\,dw = \bar G(c - a) - \bar G(c - b)$, where $F$ is the distribution function of the noise and $\bar G$ an antiderivative, $\bar G(y) = y\Phi(y/\sigma) + \sigma\phi(y/\sigma)$ for Gaussian noise. With $\tau = 10^{-4}$, a layer of width $\omega = 10^{-3}\sigma$, a thousandth of a noise standard deviation, and the noisy coverage set to $0.9$, the latent coverage is $0.870$, $0.820$, $0.807$ and $0.801$ at $\sigma = 0.1$, $0.03$, $0.01$ and $0.001$ for Gaussian noise, $0.876$, $0.822$, $0.807$ and $0.801$ for uniform noise of standard deviation $\sigma$, and $0.809$, $0.751$, $0.736$ and $0.729$ for the centred exponential noise $\sigma(E_1 - 1)$, against the limits $0.8$, $0.8$ and $1 - \mathrm{e}/10 = 0.72817$.

\section{Calibration with unknown, heterogeneous noise}\label{sec:s-calib}

This section completes the proofs of \S~\ref{M-sec:procedure}.

\subsection{Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank} and the ranks of Table~\ref{M-tab:map}}\label{sec:s-rank}

\emph{The converse in Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank}.} Let $p < \Psi_{\E}(q)$ and take $\epsilon$, $\eta$ and $Y = a + \eta - E_1$ as in \S~\ref{sec:s-oneend}. For $s > 0$ let every unit have the log-concave latent law $G$ of $W = 1 + sY$ and the noise $s\epsilon$. Then $\pr(|W| \le 1) < q$, so $r = Q_q(|W|) > 1$, and $\pr(|V_i| < r) \ge \pr(|V_i| \le 1) > p$ for small $s$. The indicators $1(|V_i| < r)$ are independent with a common success probability above $p$, so the latent reliability $\pr(N \le k - 1)$ is at most $\pr\{\Bin(K, p) \le k - 1\}$. Letting $p \uparrow \Psi_{\E}(q)$ gives the claim, since the binomial probability is continuous in $p$.

\emph{Sharpness.} Let $\ell \in (0, 1)$ and let $k_K$ be the smallest rank with $\pr\{\Bin(K, \ell) \le k_K - 1\} \ge 1 - \delta$. By minimality,
\begin{align*}
1 - \delta \le \pr\{\Bin(K, \ell) \le k_K - 1\} &< 1 - \delta + \pr\{\Bin(K, \ell) = k_K - 1\} \\
&\le 1 - \delta + \max_j\pr\{\Bin(K, \ell) = j\},
\end{align*}
and the largest binomial probability is of order $K^{-1/2}$. With $\ell = \Psi_{\E}(q)$, the supremum over the laws of the converse of the latent reliability of the rank $k_K$ is $\pr\{\Bin(K, \Psi_{\E}(q)) \le k_K - 1\}$, which tends to $1 - \delta$; the exact values in \S~\ref{sec:s-reliability} show the approach from above. This makes the rank sharp where the valid rank is computed from $p_{\E}(q) = \Psi_{\E}(q)$, that is for Gaussian and symmetric unimodal noise. For mean-zero log-concave noise the rank is computed from the certified sufficient level, which exceeds the certified lower bound on $\Psi_{\E}(q)$; at the centred exponential law its reliability then tends to one, not to $1 - \delta$, and the smallest valid rank is known only to lie in the range of Table~\ref{M-tab:map}.

\emph{Symmetric unimodal latent laws: the first row of Table~\ref{M-tab:map}.} If $W$ is symmetric and unimodal about $0$, then for every real $c$ the interval $[c - s, c + s]$ carries at most the mass of $[-s, s]$, so $\pr(|W + e| \le s \mid e) \le \pr(|W| \le s)$ for any noise $e$ independent of $W$, and integrating over $e$ gives $\pr(|V_i| \le s) \le \pr(|W| \le s)$ for every unit, whatever its noise law and scale. For $s < r = Q_q(|W|)$ this is below $q$, so $\pr(|V_i| < r) \le q$ and the proof of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank} gives the usual rank, level $q$, for any independent and heterogeneous noise; it cannot be lowered, since noise-free scores are allowed. This is the one-dimensional case of \citet{anderson1955}.

\emph{When a valid rank exists.} A rank $k \le K$ with $\pr\{\Bin(K, \ell) \le k - 1\} \ge 1 - \delta$ exists if and only if $1 - \ell^K \ge 1 - \delta$, that is $K \ge \log\delta/\log\ell$. At $q = 0.9$ and $\delta = 0.05$ this gives $K \ge 29$ for the Gaussian and symmetric unimodal levels $0.9008035$ and $0.9017873$, $K \ge 31$ for the sufficient log-concave level $0.906$, and $K \ge 59$ and $K \ge 80$ for the levels $0.95$ and $1 - 0.1/\mathrm{e}$ without a latent shape assumption. All ranks in the main paper are computed from the exact binomial distribution function, with ties within $10^{-10}$ of $1 - \delta$ rechecked at $50$ digits.

\emph{Unimodal latent laws: the last two rows of Table~\ref{M-tab:map}.} The proof of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank} uses only the transfer for one unit. With the transfer of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:latentshape}, which needs noisy level $1 - \beta_{\E}(1 - q)$, that is $(1 + q)/2$ for median-zero noise and $1 - (1 - q)/\mathrm{e}$ for mean-zero log-concave noise, it gives the ranks of the last two rows: if $\pr(|W| \le s) < q$ then $\pr(|V_i| \le s) < 1 - \beta_{\E}(1 - q)$, for every latent law. These ranks are the smallest valid for unimodal latent laws with mean zero. Let $p < 1 - \beta_{\E}(1 - q)$, and take the construction of \S~\ref{sec:s-latentshape} with the layer mass $s$ chosen in $1 - q - \tau < s < (1 - \tau - p)/\beta$, which is possible for small $\tau$ and $\gamma'$. The latent coverage of $[-1, 1]$ is then $m = 1 - s - \tau < q$, so $r = Q_q(|W|) > 1$, and $\pr(|V_i| < r) \ge \pr(|V_i| \le 1) > p$ for small $\sigma$; as above, the reliability of rank $k$ is at most $\pr\{\Bin(K, p) \le k - 1\}$.

\emph{One-sided intervals.} For $(-\infty, T]$ with $T = V_{(k)}$, the $k$th smallest signed score, let $r = F_W^{-1}(q)$ and $N = \#\{i : V_i < r\}$; then $\pr(W_{\rm new} \le T \mid \mathcal D) \ge q$ if and only if $N \le k - 1$. By Proposition~\ref{M-prop:general}, applied to $Y = (W - s)/\sigma_i$ for $s < r$, $\pr(V_i \le s) \le \Psi_{\E}(q)$, so $\pr(V_i < r) \le \Psi_{\E}(q)$, which needs only concavity of $\log F_W$; the converse of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:general} gives the matching lower bound. The smallest valid rank is therefore the smallest $k$ with $\pr\{\Bin(K, \Psi_{\E}(q)) \le k - 1\} \ge 1 - \delta$, for every noise class with $\beta_{\E} > 0$: at $q = 0.9$ the level is $0.9008035$ for Gaussian noise, $0.9017873$ for symmetric unimodal noise and in $(0.90517, 0.9052)$ for mean-zero log-concave noise. \emph{Asymmetric intervals.} Bi-log-concavity allows skewed latent laws, for which an interval $[-T_L, T_R]$ may be shorter than $[-T, T]$. Split the latent failure as $\beta_L + \beta_R = 1 - q$, fixed in advance, and let $T_R$ be the order statistic of the signed scores $V_i$ at the one-sided rank for latent level $1 - \beta_R$ and reliability $1 - \delta/2$, and $T_L$ that of the $-V_i$ for $1 - \beta_L$. The right end uses concavity of $\log F_W$ and the left end concavity of $\log(1 - F_W)$, so for $W \in \B$ both ends hold, each with probability at least $1 - \delta/2$, and the union bound gives latent coverage $q$ with probability at least $1 - \delta$; each end is exact, and only the union bound and the choice of split are conservative. We have not studied the widths of this rule.

Without a shape assumption the one-sided level is $1 - \beta_{\E}(1 - q)$: it suffices because $W > t$ and $e \ge 0$ give $W + e > t$, and a layer of mass slightly above $1 - q$ just above $t$, with noise whose probability of being nonnegative is close to $\beta_{\E}$, has latent coverage below $q$ and noisy coverage close to $1 - \beta_{\E}(1 - q)$.

\emph{Proposition~\ref{M-prop:fail}.} The proof in the main text uses only that $\Psi_{\N}(q) > q$ for every $q \in (0, 1)$: for $q \le \mathrm{e}^{-1}$, $\Psi_{\N}(q) = 1/2 > q$; for $q > \mathrm{e}^{-1}$, $\Gamma(q, s) \to q$ as $s \to 0$ and $\Gamma(q, \cdot)$ increases near $0$, by the sign of $\partial_s\Gamma$ in \S~\ref{sec:s-gauss-exact}. The converse construction above, with Gaussian noise and $q < p < \Psi_{\N}(q)$, then gives $\pr(|V_i| < r) > p$, and the law of large numbers does the rest. If $k_K = Kq + z\{Kq(1 - q)\}^{1/2} + O(1)$, as for the usual rank with $z$ the standard normal $(1 - \delta)$-quantile, and $H = \pr(|V_i| < r)$, the normal approximation gives the latent reliability $\Phi[z - K^{1/2}(H - q)/\{q(1 - q)\}^{1/2}]$, which is $1/2$ at $K = z^2q(1 - q)/(H - q)^2$; with $H = \Psi_{\N}(q)$ this is $2.2 \times 10^4$, $3.8 \times 10^5$ and $5.1 \times 10^6$ at $q = 0.8$, $0.9$ and $0.95$, close to the exact crossings below $0.5$ found by scanning every integer $K$, $K = 22{,}177$, $376{,}330$ and $5{,}080{,}045$.

\subsection{Latent laws that differ between units}\label{sec:s-hetero}

\emph{A general form of Corollary~\ref{M-cor:hetero}.} Without the quantile condition, let $c_i = \pr(|W_i| < r_0)$. For $s < r_0$ and every $q' > c_i$, $\pr(|W_i| \le s) \le c_i < q'$, and the transfer gives $\pr(|V_i| \le s) \le p_{\E}(q')$; hence $\pi_i = \pr(|V_i| < r_0) \le \bar p_i = \inf_{q' > c_i}p_{\E}(q')$. The number $N$ of the proof of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank} is a sum of independent Bernoulli variables with probabilities $\pi_i \le \bar p_i$, so it is stochastically smaller than the sum with probabilities $\bar p_i$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:bernoulli} below
\[
\pr_{\mathcal D}\{\pr(|W_{\rm new}| \le T \mid \mathcal D) \ge q\} \ge \pr\{\Bin(K, \bar\pi) \le k - 1\}, \qquad \bar\pi = K^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^K\bar p_i,
\]
whenever $k \ge K\bar\pi + 1$, by \citet[Theorem~4]{hoeffding1956}; Lemma~\ref{lem:bernoulli} below gives a self-contained proof when $k \ge K\bar\pi + 2$. Here $p_{\E}(q')$ is available for $q' > \mathrm{e}^{-1}$ in the Gaussian and symmetric unimodal classes; elsewhere take $\bar p_i = 1$. If the rank is that of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank}, the bound is at least $1 - \delta$ whenever $\bar\pi \le p_{\E}(q)$, which is the precise sense in which slack is absorbed. Under the condition of Corollary~\ref{M-cor:hetero} its proof gives $\pi_i \le p_{\E}(q)$ directly, and the bound of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank} needs no condition on $k$. A few calibration units that are easier than the new one, with $\bar p_i > p_{\E}(q)$, are absorbed if the others have slack, $\bar p_i < p_{\E}(q)$. The $c_i$ are not identifiable from the data, so this form matters for interpretation, not for computing a rank. The extra unit in $k \ge K\bar\pi + 2$ is harmless: the ranks of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank} exceed $K\bar\pi$ by about $z\{K\bar\pi(1 - \bar\pi)\}^{1/2}$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:mode}
Let $S = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ with independent $X_i \sim$ Bernoulli$(p_i)$, $\mu = \sum_ip_i$ and $a_k = \pr(S = k)$. If $j \ge \mu$ is an integer, then $a_{j+1} \le a_j$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $n$, $j$ and $\mu \le j$, and let $\Delta(p) = a_{j+1} - a_j$ on the compact set $P_\mu = \{p \in [0, 1]^n : \sum_ip_i = \mu\}$. Fix a pair $(i, l)$, let $U$ be the sum of the other $n - 2$ variables, with $u_k = \pr(U = k)$, and write $s = p_i + p_l$ and $\varpi = p_ip_l$. Then $a_k = (1 - s)u_k + su_{k-1} + \varpi(u_k - 2u_{k-1} + u_{k-2})$, so with $s$ fixed $\Delta = \alpha(s) + \gamma\varpi$, where $\gamma$ depends only on $U$. On the segment $\{p_i + p_l = s\}$, $\varpi$ is largest at $p_i = p_l$ and smallest at the endpoints, where one of the two is $0$ or $1$.

Among the maximizers of $\Delta$ on $P_\mu$, a nonempty closed set, take one with the smallest $\sum_ip_i^2$. Suppose that two of its coordinates lie in $(0, 1)$ and are unequal. If $\gamma < 0$, moving to an endpoint of the segment strictly increases $\Delta$, contradicting maximality; if $\gamma \ge 0$, setting both to $s/2$ does not decrease $\Delta$ and strictly decreases $\sum_ip_i^2$, contradicting the choice. So the fractional coordinates of this maximizer are all equal, to $\alpha$ say, and $S = m + B$, with $m$ coordinates equal to $1$, $B \sim \Bin(r, \alpha)$, and $m + r\alpha = \mu \le j$. Let $i = j - m \ge r\alpha \ge 0$. If $r = 0$ or $i \ge r$, then $a_{j+1} = 0$. Otherwise $a_{j+1}/a_j = (r - i)\alpha/\{(i + 1)(1 - \alpha)\}$, which is at most $1$ because $i \ge r\alpha > r\alpha - (1 - \alpha)$. Hence $\max_{P_\mu}\Delta \le 0$.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:mode} also follows from the theorem of \citet{darroch1964} on the mode of $S$ together with the log-concavity of its probabilities; the proof above is self-contained.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bernoulli}
In the setting of Lemma~\ref{lem:mode}, let $\bar p = \mu/n$. If $c$ is an integer with $c \ge n\bar p + 1$, then $\pr(S \le c) \ge \pr\{\Bin(n, \bar p) \le c\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Replace $(p_i, p_l)$ by their mean and let $R$ be the sum of the other variables. The law of $X_i + X_l$ gains $d = (p_i - p_l)^2/4 \ge 0$ at $0$ and at $2$ and loses $2d$ at $1$, so $\pr(S \le c)$ changes by $d\{F_R(c) - 2F_R(c - 1) + F_R(c - 2)\} = d\{\pr(R = c) - \pr(R = c - 1)\} \le 0$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:mode} for $R$ with $j = c - 1$, since the mean of $R$ is at most $n\bar p \le c - 1$. Each replacement keeps $\sum_ip_i$. Averaging the largest and smallest coordinates reduces $\sum_i(p_i - \bar p)^2$ by $(p_{\max} - p_{\min})^2/2$, at least $1/(2n)$ times its value, so iterating drives $p$ to $(\bar p, \ldots, \bar p)$; as $\pr(S \le c)$ is continuous in $p$ and never increases along the way, the limit gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\citet[Theorem~4]{hoeffding1956} gives the same comparison under the weaker condition $c \ge n\bar p$; on $20{,}000$ random probability vectors with $n \le 40$ the inequality held under that condition too, with largest violation $9 \times 10^{-16}$, rounding.

\emph{Normalized scores.} Let $s_i > 0$ be known scales fixed before calibration, such as functions of the training fit or of area sizes, and use the scores $|V_i|/s_i$ and the interval $[-s_{\rm new}T, s_{\rm new}T]$. Then $W_i/s_i \in \B$ and $e_i/s_i$ has a law in $\E$, because both classes are closed under scaling, and the proof of Corollary~\ref{M-cor:hetero} applies with $W_i/s_i$ and $W_{\rm new}/s_{\rm new}$ in place of $W_i$ and $W_{\rm new}$. Latent laws that differ only in a known scale therefore give Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank} exactly; this is the usual normalized conformal score and covers heteroscedastic latent residuals.

\emph{Why a link between units is needed.} Suppose that the new unit is a random draw from a heterogeneous population, so that $W_{\rm new}$ follows the mixture of the calibration laws. Point masses lie in $\B$, and their mixtures are arbitrary laws, so Proposition~\ref{M-prop:latentshape} applies and the guarantee is the shape-free one. Concretely, let each unit be of one of two types, with latent point masses at $0$ and at $1 + \eta$, the second type with probability $w$ slightly above $1 - q$, and let the noise be $N(0, \sigma^2)$ with $\sigma$ small. Then $r_0 = Q_q(|W_{\rm new}|) = 1 + \eta$, while $\pr(|V_i| < r_0) \to (1 - w) + w/2 = 1 - w/2$, about $(1 + q)/2$, as $\sigma \to 0$. This exceeds $\Psi_{\N}(q)$, so the latent reliability of the rank of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank} tends to zero as $K$ grows (\S~\ref{sec:s-hetero-sim}), while the shape-free rank, at level $(1 + q)/2$, stays valid. If instead the population law itself is bi-log-concave and the noise is independent of the type of unit, Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank} applies directly to it.

\subsection{Contaminated latent laws: proof of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:contam}}\label{sec:s-contam}

Let $\E$ be the Gaussian or the symmetric unimodal class, so that $\beta = \beta_{\E} = 1/2$, and write $\Psi = \Psi_{\E}$ and $\lambda = \lambda_{\E}$. By Theorem~\ref{M-thm:gauss}(iii) and Proposition~\ref{prop:su-exact}, $\Psi$ is continuous, increasing, differentiable and strictly convex on $(\mathrm{e}^{-1}, 1)$, with $\Psi \to 1/2$ at $\mathrm{e}^{-1}$ and $\Psi \to 1$ at $1$; hence $\lambda$ is increasing, differentiable and concave on $(1/2, 1)$, with $\lambda' = 1/\Psi'(\lambda)$. Let $q_\beta \in (\mathrm{e}^{-1}, 1)$ solve $\Psi'(q_\beta) = 1/2$: $q_\beta = 0.44635$ and $\Psi(q_\beta) = 0.52634$ for Gaussian noise, $q_\beta = 0.42611$ and $\Psi(q_\beta) = 0.52267$ for symmetric unimodal noise (double precision). The condition $q \ge 0.53$ of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:contam} ensures $q \ge \Psi(q_\beta)$ in both classes.

\emph{Sufficiency.} Fix $t > 0$, $\xi \in [0, 1 - q)$, and write $W$ as $W_0$ with probability $1 - \xi$ and $W_H$ with probability $\xi$, through a label independent of the noise $e$, with $W_0 \sim G \in \B$ and $W_H \sim H$. Let $P_0 = \pr(|W_0 + e| \le t)$ and $x = \pr(|W_H + e| \le t)$, so that the noisy coverage is $P = (1 - \xi)P_0 + \xi x$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:convex}, $\pr(|W_0| \le t) \ge \lambda(P_0)$, with $\lambda(P_0) = 0$ for $P_0 \le 1/2$. Without a shape assumption, $\pr(|W_H| \le t) \ge f(x) = \max\{0, 1 - 2(1 - x)\}$, because $|W_H| > t$ and $e$ of the same sign as $W_H$ give $|W_H + e| > t$, and $\pr(e \ge 0), \pr(e \le 0) \ge 1/2$. Hence the latent coverage is at least
\[
J_P(x) = (1 - \xi)\lambda\{P_0(x)\} + \xi f(x), \qquad P_0(x) = (P - \xi x)/(1 - \xi),
\]
minimized over the $x \in [0, 1]$ with $P_0(x) \in [0, 1]$. As $\lambda$ and $f$ are nondecreasing, this minimum is nondecreasing in $P$, so it suffices to show that it exceeds $q$ for $P \in (p_\xi(q), 1 - \xi/2)$; note that $p_\xi(q) < 1 - \xi/2$ because $\Psi < 1$ on $(0, 1)$. On $x \in [0, 1/2]$, $f = 0$ and $J_P$ is nonincreasing, so its minimum there is at $x = 1/2$, which is feasible since $P \le 1 - \xi/2$. On $x \in [1/2, 1]$, $P_0(x)$ runs over $[P_0(1), P_0(1/2)]$. Since $P > p_\xi(q)$, $q_0 = q/(1 - \xi) < 1$ and $\Psi(q_0) \ge q_0$,
\[
P_0(1) = \frac{P - \xi}{1 - \xi} > \Psi(q_0) - \frac{\xi}{2(1 - \xi)} \ge \frac{q - \xi/2}{1 - \xi} \ge q,
\]
the last step because $q \ge 1/2$. So $P_0(1) > q \ge \Psi(q_\beta) > 1/2$ and $P_0(1/2) < 1$, $\lambda$ is concave on $[P_0(1), P_0(1/2)]$, $J_P$ is concave on $[1/2, 1]$, and its minimum there is at an end. By concavity, $\lambda\{P_0(1/2)\} - \lambda\{P_0(1)\} \le \lambda'\{P_0(1)\}\xi/\{2(1 - \xi)\}$, and $\lambda'\{P_0(1)\} = 1/\Psi'[\lambda\{P_0(1)\}] \le 1/\Psi'(q_\beta) = 2$ because $\lambda\{P_0(1)\} > q_\beta$ and $\Psi'$ is nondecreasing. Hence
\[
J_P(1) - J_P(1/2) \ge \xi - (1 - \xi)\,\lambda'\{P_0(1)\}\,\frac{\xi}{2(1 - \xi)} \ge 0,
\]
and the minimum of $J_P$ is $J_P(1/2) = (1 - \xi)\lambda[\{P - \xi/2\}/(1 - \xi)]$, which exceeds $(1 - \xi)\lambda\{\Psi(q_0)\} = q$. For $\xi \ge 1 - q$, the level $(1 + q)/2 = 1 - \beta(1 - q)$ suffices for every law, by Proposition~\ref{M-prop:latentshape}.

\emph{Sharpness.} Let $\xi < 1 - q$, $q_0 = q/(1 - \xi)$, $a_0 = \log(1/q_0)$, and let $\epsilon^*$ attain $\Psi(q_0)$: $s^*Z$ for Gaussian noise (Theorem~\ref{M-thm:gauss}), uniform on $[-r, r]$ for symmetric unimodal noise; in both cases $\pr(\epsilon^* < 0) = 1/2$ and $\epsilon^*$ has no atom. For $\eta, \eta' > 0$ and $s > 0$ let $G$ be the law of $t + s(a_0 + \eta - E_1)$, which is log-concave, let $H$ be the point mass at $t + s\eta'$, and let the noise be $s\epsilon^*$. The latent coverage is at most $(1 - \xi)q_0\mathrm{e}^{-\eta} < q$, since $t + s\eta' > t$. As $s \to 0$, the noisy coverage tends to $(1 - \xi)E\{\min(1, q_0\mathrm{e}^{-\eta - \epsilon^*})\} + \xi\pr(\epsilon^* < -\eta')$ (\S~\ref{sec:s-oneend}), and as $\eta, \eta' \to 0$ to $(1 - \xi)\Psi(q_0) + \xi/2 = p_\xi(q)$. So no level below $p_\xi(q)$ suffices. For $\xi \ge 1 - q$, take $G$ the point mass at $0$ and $H$ with mass $w$ at $t + s\eta'$ and $1 - w$ at $0$, with $\xi w$ slightly above $1 - q$; the latent coverage is $1 - \xi w < q$ and the noisy coverage tends to $1 - \xi w/2$, close to $(1 + q)/2$.

\emph{Monotonicity in $\xi$.} With $q_0 = q/(1 - \xi)$, $\partial_\xi p_\xi(q) = 1/2 - \{\Psi(q_0) - q_0\Psi'(q_0)\}$. By convexity of $\Psi$ on $[\mathrm{e}^{-1}, q_0]$ and $\Psi(\mathrm{e}^{-1}+) = 1/2$, the tangent at $q_0$ lies below $\Psi$ at $\mathrm{e}^{-1}$, so $\Psi(q_0) - q_0\Psi'(q_0) \le 1/2 - \Psi'(q_0)/\mathrm{e} < 1/2$, and $p_\xi(q)$ increases strictly in $\xi$; at $\xi = 1 - q$ it equals $q + (1 - q)/2 = (1 + q)/2$, so it is continuous there.

\emph{The rank.} The proof of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank} uses the transfer for one unit at thresholds $s < r = Q_q(|W|)$; with the transfer above, $\pr(|V_i| < r) \le p_\xi(q)$ for every $W_i \in \B_\xi$, and the converse and sharpness follow from the laws of the sharpness part, used in every unit, as in \S~\ref{sec:s-rank}; Corollary~\ref{M-cor:hetero} carries over unchanged, since $\B_\xi$ increases with $\xi$. Since $\pr\{\Bin(K, \ell) \le k - 1\} = \pr\{\mathrm{Beta}(k, K + 1 - k) > \ell\}$ decreases in $\ell$, rank $k$ is valid over $\B_\xi$ if and only if $p_\xi(q)$ is at most the $\delta$-quantile of Beta$(k, K + 1 - k)$, and the contamination it tolerates is the root of that equation in $\xi$. If calibration and new units are independent draws from a population whose latent law is in $\B_\xi$, and the noise is independent of $W$ given the component label with law in $\E$, the argument applies to a random unit.

\emph{Scope.} For $q < 0.53$ the closed form can be too low and is not claimed: when $P_0(1) \le 1/2$, the jump of $\lambda$ at $1/2$ adds a candidate to the minimum of $J_P$; at $q = 0.4$ and $\xi = 0.3$ the closed form gives $0.5747$, a direct minimization $0.65$. For mean-zero log-concave noise the sufficiency argument holds with the certified transfer, but its evaluation needs convexity of the two-sided transfer, which is not established; the construction above with centred exponential noise gives the lower bound $(1 - \xi)\max_{\sigma}E[\min\{1, q_0\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma(E_1 - 1)}\}] + \xi(1 - \mathrm{e}^{-1})$ on any valid level. The same argument with the one-sided transfer gives the same level for one-sided intervals.

\subsection{Centred residuals: proof of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:centred}}\label{sec:s-centred}

Coverages in the definition of $p^\zeta_{\E}(q)$ are of the open interval $(-1, 1)$. For continuous noise the noisy coverage is the same on the closed interval, and a log-concave law that is not a point mass has no atom; a point mass with $\pr(|W| < 1) \le q < 1$ lies at $|w_0| \ge 1$ and has noisy coverage at most $1/2$.

\emph{Part (i).} Let $r = Q_q(|W|) > 0$ and $N = \#\{i : |V_i| < r\}$, so that the latent coverage is at least $q$ if and only if $N \le k - 1$, as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank}. Since $r$ is the lower quantile, $\pr(|W| < r) \le q$. The law of $W/r$ is log-concave with $|E(W/r)| \le \zeta$ and $\pr(|W/r| < 1) \le q$, and $e_i/r$ has a law in the class, which is closed under scaling; so $\pr(|V_i| < r) = \pr(|W/r + e_i/r| < 1) \le p^\zeta_{\E}(q)$ unit by unit, by the definition of $p^\zeta_{\E}(q)$ as a supremum, and $N$ is stochastically smaller than $\Bin\{K, p^\zeta_{\E}(q)\}$. The transfer is applied at $r$ itself rather than at $s < r$, because at threshold $s$ the bound on the mean is $\zeta r/s > \zeta$; this is why the definition uses open intervals. With latent laws $G_i$ that differ between units, the same holds when $G_i \in \LC$, $Q_q(|W_i|) \ge r_0$ and $|E(W_i)| \le \zeta r_0$ for every $i$, with $r_0 = Q_q(|W_{\rm new}|)$, as in Corollary~\ref{M-cor:hetero}.

\emph{Part (ii).} As $\LC \subset \B$, Lemma~\ref{lem:twoend} with Lemma~\ref{lem:convex} gives $p^\zeta_{\E}(q) \le \Psi_{\E}(q)$. The law $W = 1 + s(a + \eta - E_1)$ of \S~\ref{sec:s-oneend}, with $a = \log(1/q) < 1$, has mean $1 - s(1 - a - \eta) \in (-1, 1)$ for small $s$ and $\eta$, so it is feasible for every $\zeta \ge 1$, and its noisy coverage tends to $E\{\min(1, q\mathrm{e}^{-\eta - \epsilon})\}$; hence $p^\zeta_{\E}(q) = \Psi_{\E}(q)$ for $\zeta \ge 1$. For the strict lower bound, let $W$ have density $f$ proportional to $\mathrm{e}^{\beta w}$ on a segment $[a', b']$ with $a' < -1 < 1 < b'$, mean zero and $\pr(|W| < 1) = q$; for $\beta = 0$ the uniform law on $[-1/q, 1/q]$ qualifies, and by continuity so does a placement of the tilted law for small $\beta > 0$. As $f$ is smooth near $\pm 1$, with noise of variance $v$,
\[
\pr(|W + e| < 1) - q = \tfrac12 v\{f'(1) - f'(-1)\} + O(v^2)
\]
for Gaussian and for uniform noise, and $f'(1) - f'(-1) = \beta\{f(1) - f(-1)\} > 0$. So $p^0_{\E}(q) > q$, and $p^\zeta_{\E}(q)$ is nondecreasing in $\zeta$. For $q > 1 - \mathrm{e}^{-1}$ only means in $(-1, 1)$ matter: if $E(W) = \mu \ge 1$, then $W + e$ is log-concave with mean $\mu$ for Gaussian or uniform noise, so $\pr(|W + e| < 1) \le \pr(W + e < \mu) \le 1 - \mathrm{e}^{-1} < q$ by fact 4 of \S~\ref{sec:s-prelim}.

\emph{Part (iii).} For a fixed noise law the noisy coverage is linear in the law of $W$; for symmetric unimodal noise $\epsilon = RU$, with $U$ uniform on $[-1, 1]$ and $R \ge 0$ (\S~\ref{sec:s-su}), so the supremum over the class is over uniform laws, and for Gaussian noise over the scale. With a continuous noise law, $g(w) = \pr(|w + e| < 1)$ is bounded and continuous, and the constraints are $\int f_j\,d\mu \ge 0$ with $f_1 = q - 1_{(-1, 1)}$, which is upper semicontinuous because the interval is open, and $f_2 = \zeta - w$, $f_3 = \zeta + w$. On a compact interval the localization theorem of \citet{fradelizi2006} shows that the supremum of $\int g\,d\mu$ over the log-concave probabilities satisfying these constraints is attained at a point mass or at a law with density $\mathrm{e}^{-V}$ on a segment, $V$ the maximum of at most three affine functions. The reduction to compact support is as in Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}, except that the mean must be kept: a feasible law with $\pr(|W| < 1) = q$ is first dilated slightly about its mean, which lowers its latent coverage strictly, then conditioned on $[-M, M]$ and translated back to its mean; for large $M$ the result is feasible and its noisy coverage is arbitrarily close. Both bounds on the mean are active only when $\zeta = 0$, where they impose one equation, so the degrees-of-freedom count in the proof of the localization theorem leaves at most two affine pieces; the computation uses two pieces, and a re-optimization over three pieces changed no value by more than $6 \times 10^{-11}$.

\emph{Computation.} A two-piece law on a segment is placed with its mean in $[0, \min(\zeta, 1))$, and scaled so that its latent coverage is exactly $q$; its distribution function, mean and $E\{(x - W)_+\}$ have closed forms, evaluated in log space. Noisy coverage under uniform noise is a combination of $E\{(x - W)_+\}$, and under Gaussian noise a Gauss--Legendre quadrature split at the kinks, accurate to $10^{-15}$ against $30$-digit quadrature. A multistart Nelder--Mead search over the six parameters gives each value, and each maximizer is rechecked in $30$-digit arithmetic. An adversarial search over $3700$ log-concave laws outside the family, standard laws and random piecewise log-affine laws with $3$ to $40$ pieces, each optimized over its mean, scale and the noise scale, found none above the reported levels. The values are therefore attained, hence necessary; that they are the suprema is numerical, without a certificate.

\subsection{A shifted new residual}\label{sec:s-shift}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:shift}
Let $W$ be log-concave with standard deviation $\varsigma$, let $\pr(|W| \le t) \ge q'$ and $|\Delta| \le \rho\varsigma$. Then $\pr(|W + \Delta| \le t) \ge q' - \rho$. Conversely, for symmetric unimodal noise, $q_0 > \mathrm{e}^{-1}$ and $p = \Psi_{\E}(q_0)$, there are log-concave $W_n$, with standard deviations $\varsigma_n$, and noise laws in the class such that, as $n \to \infty$, the noisy coverage $\pr(|W_n + e_n| \le t)$ tends to $p$, the latent coverage $\pr(|W_n| \le t)$ tends to $q_0 = \lambda_{\E}(p)$, and $\pr(|W_n + \rho\varsigma_n| \le t) \to q_0\mathrm{e}^{-\rho}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A shift by $\Delta > 0$ moves the window $[-t, t]$ to $[-t - \Delta, t - \Delta]$, which loses at most the mass of $(t - \Delta, t]$, at most $\Delta\sup f$. For a log-concave density $\varsigma\sup f \le 1$, with equality for the exponential law \citep{bobkov2015}, so the loss is at most $\rho$. For the converse let $b = u^{-1}\log(1/q_0)$ and $W = t + b - E_u$, so that $\varsigma = 1/u$, $\pr(W \le t) = q_0$ and $\pr(|W| \le t) = q_0(1 - \mathrm{e}^{-2ut})$, and let the noise be uniform on $[-r/u, r/u]$ with $r$ the maximizer of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:classes}(i) at $q_0$. As $ut \to \infty$ the left end does not matter, the latent coverage tends to $q_0$ and, by scaling, the noisy coverage to $E\{\min(1, q_0\mathrm{e}^{-rU})\} = \Psi_{\E}(q_0) = p$, with $U$ uniform on $[-1, 1]$; and $\pr(W + \rho/u \le t) = \pr(E_u \ge b + \rho/u) = q_0\mathrm{e}^{-\rho}$.
\end{proof}

The loss $q_0(1 - \mathrm{e}^{-\rho}) = q_0\rho + O(\rho^2)$ shows that the linear order of the bound cannot be improved. At $q = 0.9$, the latent coverage guaranteed by noisy coverage $0.9$ under symmetric unimodal noise is thus between $0.8881$ and $0.8892$ for $\rho = 0.01$, and between $0.798$ and $0.813$ for $\rho = 0.1$. Measured relative to $t$ rather than to $\varsigma$, a shift has no nontrivial bound: point masses just inside the threshold satisfy every noisy constraint. A rule that picks the rank for latent level $q + \rho$ would protect against such shifts, but it is too coarse to be useful: under symmetric unimodal noise at $K = 1000$ it gives the ranks $918$, $927$, $945$ and $962$ for $\rho = 0$, $0.01$, $0.03$ and $0.05$, and the last equals the rank without a shape assumption.

\section{What a known noise variance adds}\label{sec:s-known}

When the noise is Gaussian with known variance $D$, the noisy threshold can be widened instead of its rank raised. This section treats that problem, which the main paper mentions only in its discussion. After scaling the threshold $t$ to $1$, write $x = D/t^2$, let $Z \sim N(0,1)$ be independent of $W$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R}
R_{p,q}(x) = \sup\{ Q_q(|W|) : W \in \LC,\ \pr(|W + x^{1/2}Z| \le 1) \ge p \},
\end{equation}
with $R_{p,q}(x) = -\infty$ if nothing is feasible. If calibration certifies noisy coverage $p$ at threshold $t$, then $t\,R_{p,q}(D/t^2)$ is the smallest radius that guarantees latent coverage $q$ for every log-concave latent law; $R^{\B}_{p,q} \ge R_{p,q}$ is defined with $\B$ in place of $\LC$. A reduction to log-affine laws on segments makes $R_{p,q}$ computable (Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}). The behaviour for small $x$ is governed by the one-sided constant $c_q = \sup\{F_Y^{-1}(q) : Y \in \LC,\ \pr(Y + Z \le 0) \ge q\}$, attained along exponential tails and the same over $\B$; ball arithmetic gives $c_{0.8} = 0.04607$, $c_{0.9} = 0.01906$ and $c_{0.95} = 0.00841$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:small}
For every $q \in (0,1)$, every $x > 0$ and every $W \in \B$ with $\pr(|W + x^{1/2}Z| \le 1) \ge q$, $Q_q(|W|) \le 1 + c_q x^{1/2}$. Moreover $R_{q,q}(x) = 1 + c_q x^{1/2} + o(x^{1/2})$ as $x \to 0$, and likewise for $R^{\B}_{q,q}$.
\end{theorem}

The widening is therefore of the order of the noise standard deviation; at $q = 0.9$ it is below $1.16\%$ of the radius for every $W \in \B$ (\S~\ref{sec:s-feasible}). A mean away from the boundary reduces the order to $D$: for a mean-zero latent law $Q_q(|W|) \le (t^2 + D)^{1/2}$, via the heat equation for quantiles \citep{steinbrecher2008}, an order that centred log-affine laws attain, and the order $D^{1/2}$ returns only when the mean is within a bounded number of noise standard deviations of the boundary (Theorems~\ref{thm:mean} and~\ref{thm:transition}); compare \citet{jochmans2024} for a fixed smooth law. A noisy level of at least $\Psi_{\N}(q)$ removes the widening at every noise level (Proposition~\ref{prop:slack}). Known variances also allow shorter intervals with heterogeneous units: a rule that is given the variances, assumes Gaussian noise and computes the radius for log-concave latent laws allowed by a single constraint on the average noisy kernel (HetLDC, \S~\ref{sec:s-kv}) was about $10\%$ shorter than the rank of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank} at $K = 110$ under Gaussian noise, the only setting where it was run; its implementation computes an upper bound of the radius in double precision, which is not a certificate.

\subsection{Reduction to log-affine laws}\label{sec:s-reduction}

Write $g_x(w) = \pr(|w + x^{1/2}Z| \le 1)$, so that the constraint in~(\ref{eq:R}) reads $E\{g_x(W)\} \ge p$, and let $\mathcal F$ be the family of point masses and of laws with density proportional to $\mathrm{e}^{\beta w}$ on a segment.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:reduction}
$R_{p,q}(x) = \sup\{Q_q(|W|) : W \in \mathcal F,\ E\{g_x(W)\} \ge p\}$. The same holds for any continuous constraint kernel that vanishes at $\pm\infty$, in particular for the average kernel of \S~\ref{sec:s-kv}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $\mathcal F \subset \LC$ it suffices to bound the supremum over $\LC$. Let $W \in \LC$ satisfy $E\{g_x(W)\} \ge p$ and let $s < Q_q(|W|)$, so that $\pr(|W| < s) \le q - \gamma$ for some $\gamma > 0$. Choose $M \ge s$ with $g_x(w) \le p$ for $|w| \ge M$, and let $W_M$ be $W$ conditioned on $|W| \le M$, which is log-concave. With $\varepsilon_M = \pr(|W| > M)$, the conditioning removes only mass on which the kernel is at most $p$, so $E\{g_x(W_M)\} \ge (p - p\varepsilon_M)/(1 - \varepsilon_M) = p$ and $\pr(|W_M| < s) \le (q - \gamma)/(1 - \varepsilon_M) < q$ for $M$ large. On $[-M, M]$ the constraint function $g_x - p$ is continuous, and $\mu \mapsto -\mu(-s, s)$ is linear and upper semicontinuous for weak convergence because $(-s, s)$ is open. By the localization theorem \citep*{kannan1995, fradelizi2004}, the supremum of this functional over the log-concave probability measures on $[-M, M]$ with $\int(g_x - p)\,d\mu \ge 0$ is attained at a point mass or at a law with log-affine density on a segment. Hence some member of $\mathcal F$ is feasible and has $\pr(|W| < s) < q$, that is $Q_q(|W|) \ge s$. Only continuity of the kernel and its decay at infinity were used.
\end{proof}

For $\beta > 0$ and $\sigma = x^{1/2}$, integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closed}
\begin{split}
\int_a^b\mathrm{e}^{\beta w}\Phi\Big(\frac{c - w}{\sigma}\Big)dw ={}& \frac{1}{\beta}\Big[\mathrm{e}^{\beta w}\Phi\Big(\frac{c - w}{\sigma}\Big)\Big]_a^b \\
&+ \frac{\mathrm{e}^{\beta c + \beta^2\sigma^2/2}}{\beta}\Big\{\Phi\Big(\frac{b - c - \beta\sigma^2}{\sigma}\Big) - \Phi\Big(\frac{a - c - \beta\sigma^2}{\sigma}\Big)\Big\},
\end{split}
\end{equation}
so the constraint over $\mathcal F$ is a combination of normal distribution functions. For fixed slope and length, the translations $c$ of a member of $\mathcal F$ that satisfy the constraint form an interval, because $c \mapsto \pr(|W + c + e| \le 1)$ is log-concave \citep{prekopa1973}, and $c \mapsto Q_q(|W + c|)$ is quasi-convex, so it is maximized at an end of that interval.

\subsection{The one-sided problem}\label{sec:s-onesided}

For $u > 0$ and the exponential tail $b - E_u$, independent of $Z$, let
\[
m_u(b) = \pr(b - E_u + Z \le 0) = \Phi(-b) + \mathrm{e}^{-ub + u^2/2}\Phi(b - u),
\]
which is continuous and strictly decreasing from $1$ to $0$ in $b$. Let $b_u(p)$ solve $m_u(b) = p$, and write $v_{p,q}(u) = b_u(p) - u^{-1}\log(1/q)$, $v_q = v_{q,q}$ and $k_q(u) = u^{-1} - b_u(q)$; the law $b_u(q) - E_u$ has $q$-quantile $v_q(u)$ and mean $-k_q(u)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:onesided}
Let $c_{p,q} = \sup\{F_Y^{-1}(q) : Y \in \LC,\ \pr(Y + Z \le 0) \ge p\}$ and $c_q = c_{q,q}$. Then $c_{p,q} = \sup_{u > 0}v_{p,q}(u)$, $c_q > 0$, and $q \mapsto c_q$ is nonincreasing. The same holds with $\B$, or with the class of laws with $\log F_Y$ concave, in place of $\LC$. Moreover $c_{p,q} > 0$ if and only if $p < \Psi_{\N}(q)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Exponential tails are log-concave, which gives one inequality. Conversely, let $\log F_Y$ be concave with $\pr(Y + Z \le 0) \ge p$. Let $v = F_Y^{-1}(q)$. Concavity of $\log F_Y$ allows an atom only at the left end of the support. If $Y$ has an atom there with mass at least $q$, a point mass included, then $Y \ge v$ almost surely, so $p \le \Phi(-v)$ and $v \le -\Phi^{-1}(p) = \lim_{u\to\infty}v_{p,q}(u)$. Otherwise $F_Y(v) = q$; let $u$ be the right derivative of $\log F_Y$ at $v$, a supergradient; $u > 0$, since $u = 0$ would give $F_Y \le q < 1$. By fact 5 of \S~\ref{sec:s-prelim}, $Y' = v + u^{-1}\log(1/q) - E_u$ lies stochastically below $Y$ and has $q$-quantile $v$, so $p \le \pr(Y + Z \le 0) \le m_u\{v + u^{-1}\log(1/q)\}$, and $v \le v_{p,q}(u)$ since $m_u$ decreases. For monotonicity, let $q' > q$, let $Y$ be feasible at $(q', q')$ with $v = F_Y^{-1}(q')$, and put $\rho = (q' - q)/(1 - q)$. Then $Y$ conditioned on $Y \ge F_Y^{-1}(\rho)$ stays in the class: $\log(F - \rho) = h(\log F)$ with $h(z) = \log(\mathrm{e}^z - \rho)$ nondecreasing and concave, and the log survival function of the conditioned law is $\min\{0, \log(1 - F) - \log(1 - \rho)\}$, a minimum of concave functions. It satisfies the constraint at level $(q' - \rho)/(1 - \rho) = q$, because the removed mass has noisy constraint value at most one, and has $q$-quantile $v$; point masses have value $-\Phi^{-1}(q') < -\Phi^{-1}(q) \le c_q$. For small $u$, $m_u(b) \ge \mathrm{e}^{-ub + u^2/2}\Phi(b - u) \to 1$ for each fixed $b$, so $b_u(q) \to \infty$; then $\mathrm{e}^{-ub + u^2/2}\Phi(b - u) \le q \le \Phi(-b) + \mathrm{e}^{-ub + u^2/2}$ at $b = b_u(q)$ gives $ub_u(q) \to a = \log(1/q)$, so $\Phi(-b)$ and $\Phi(u - b)$ are of order $\exp\{-a^2/(3u^2)\}$, and $-ub + u^2/2 = \log q + o(u^2)$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smallu}
v_q(u) = u/2 + o(u), \qquad k_q(u) = (1 - a)/u - u/2 + o(u) \qquad (u \to 0).
\end{equation}
Finally, at $b = u^{-1}\log(1/q)$, $m_u(b) = \Phi(-a/u) + q\mathrm{e}^{u^2/2}\Phi(a/u - u) = \Gamma(q, u)$, with $a = \log(1/q)$ and $\Gamma$ as in~(\ref{M-eq:G}). As $m_u$ is strictly decreasing, $v_{p,q}(u) > 0$ if and only if $\Gamma(q, u) > p$, so $c_{p,q} > 0$ if and only if $\sup_u\Gamma(q, u) = \Psi_{\N}(q) > p$. In particular $c_q = c_{q,q} > 0$, since $\Psi_{\N}(q) > q$ (\S~\ref{sec:s-rank}).
\end{proof}

The last statement links the two sections of the main paper: the exponential tail with rate $u$ and unit noise is the extremal law of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:general} with noise scale $u$. Ball arithmetic gives $c_{0.8} = 0.04607$, $c_{0.9} = 0.01906$ and $c_{0.95} = 0.00841$ (\S~\ref{sec:s-ball}).

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:small}}\label{sec:s-small}

\emph{Upper bound.} Let $\alpha_R = \pr(W + x^{1/2}Z > 1)$ and $\alpha_L = \pr(W + x^{1/2}Z < -1)$, so that $\alpha_L + \alpha_R \le 1 - q$. The law of $Y = (W - 1)/x^{1/2}$ has $\log F_Y$ concave and $\pr(Y + Z \le 0) = 1 - \alpha_R$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:onesided}, $F_Y^{-1}(1 - \alpha_R) \le c_{1 - \alpha_R} \le c_q$, that is $\pr(W > 1 + c_qx^{1/2}) \le \alpha_R$. The same argument applied to $-W$, which uses concavity of $\log(1 - F_W)$, gives $\pr(W < -1 - c_qx^{1/2}) \le \alpha_L$, so $\pr(|W| \le 1 + c_qx^{1/2}) \ge q$.

\emph{Lower bound, for every $p \in (0, 1)$.} Fix $u > 0$, let $b_{\min} = b_u\{(1 + p)/2\}$ and, for $p' \in [p, (1 + p)/2]$, let $Y = b_u(p') - E_u$ and $W = 1 + x^{1/2}Y$, which is log-concave. With $\tau = 2x^{-1/2}$,
\[
\pr(|W + x^{1/2}Z| \le 1) = p' - \pr(Y + Z < -\tau) \ge p' - \eta_x,
\]
with $\eta_x = \mathrm{e}^{-u(\tau + b_{\min})/2} + \Phi\{-(\tau + b_{\min})/2\}$,
by splitting the event $Y + Z < -\tau$ according to whether $Z < -(\tau + b_{\min})/2$. Take $p' = p + \eta_x$, which lies in $[p, (1 + p)/2]$ for small $x$. Since $\pr(|W| \le s) \le F_W(s)$, $Q_q(|W|) \ge 1 + x^{1/2}v_{p + \eta_x, q}(u)$. As $\eta_x \to 0$ and $b_u$ is continuous, $\liminf_{x\to0}\{R_{p,q}(x) - 1\}/x^{1/2} \ge v_{p,q}(u)$ for every $u$, hence $\ge c_{p,q}$. With $p = q$ and the upper bound this proves Theorem~\ref{thm:small}; the lower bound uses log-concave laws and so holds for $R^{\B}_{q,q}$ too.

\subsection{Feasibility and the size of the widening}\label{sec:s-feasible}

The kernel $g_x$ is symmetric and maximal at $0$, so $E\{g_x(W)\} \le g_x(0) = 2\Phi(x^{-1/2}) - 1$ for every $W$, and a latent law is feasible at level $q$ only if $x^{1/2} \le 1/\Phi^{-1}\{(1 + q)/2\}$. With Theorem~\ref{thm:small}, the widening $Q_q(|W|) - 1 \le c_qx^{1/2}$ is at most $c_q/\Phi^{-1}\{(1 + q)/2\}$, which is $0.01906/1.6449 < 1.16\%$ at $q = 0.9$, for every $W \in \B$ and every noise variance. Grid maximization over $\mathcal F$, which bounds $R_{q,q}$ from below, puts the largest widening at $0.37\%$, $0.063\%$ and $0.012\%$ for $q = 0.8$, $0.9$ and $0.95$, and the grid values fall below $1$ beyond $x \approx 0.034$, $0.0062$ and $0.0012$ (numerical); grid values below $1$ do not by themselves show that shrinking the threshold is safe.

\subsection{The noisy level that removes the widening}\label{sec:s-slack}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:slack}
Let $\mathrm{e}^{-1} < q < 1$. If $p \ge \Psi_{\N}(q)$, then $R^{\B}_{p,q}(x) \le 1$ for every $x > 0$. If $p < \Psi_{\N}(q)$, then $R_{p,q}(x) > 1$ for all sufficiently small $x > 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $p \ge \Psi_{\N}(q)$ and $W \in \B$ has $\pr(|W + x^{1/2}Z| \le 1) \ge p$, Lemma~\ref{lem:convex} for Gaussian noise gives $\pr(|W| \le 1) \ge \lambda_{\N}(p) \ge q$, so $Q_q(|W|) \le 1$. If $q \le p < \Psi_{\N}(q)$, then $c_{p,q} > 0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:onesided}, and the lower bound of \S~\ref{sec:s-small} gives $R_{p,q}(x) \ge 1 + c_{p,q}x^{1/2} + o(x^{1/2}) > 1$ for small $x$; if $p < q$, $R_{p,q} \ge R_{q,q}$, which exceeds $1$ for small $x$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:small}.
\end{proof}

At $q = 0.9$ this gives the threshold $\Psi_{\N}(0.9) = 0.9008035$ stated above. In the same way noisy coverage $p$ guarantees latent coverage $\lambda_{\N}(p)$ at every noise level, $0.89918$ at $p = 0.9$, and no more for log-concave latent laws at small noise.

\subsection{The location of the mean}\label{sec:s-mean}

Let $R^{(b)}_{p,q}$ be defined as $R_{p,q}$ with the additional constraint $|E(W)| \le b$, so that the mean is at least $1 - b$ from $\pm1$. A mean away from the boundary reduces the order of the widening from $x^{1/2}$ to $x$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:mean}
Let $W \in \B$ with $|E(W)| \le M$, let $\sigma > 0$ and $t > M$, and suppose that $\pr(|W + \sigma Z| \le t) \ge q$. Then
\[
Q_q(|W|) \le M + \{(t - M)^2 + \sigma^2\}^{1/2} \le t + \frac{\sigma^2}{2(t - M)}.
\]
In particular $R^{(b)}_{p,q}(x) \le 1 + x/\{2(1 - b)\}$ for all $p \ge q$ and $x > 0$, and a mean-zero latent law has $Q_q(|W|) \le (t^2 + D)^{1/2}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mu = E(W)$. If $W$ is a point mass, $Q_q(|W|) = |\mu| \le M$. Otherwise, for $s > 0$ the law of $W + s^{1/2}Z$ is in $\B$, with mean $\mu$, distribution function $F_s$ and a positive smooth density $f_s$. For $r > \mu$ let $h = f_s(r)/F_s(r)$. The tangent of $\log F_s$ at $r$ gives $F_s(y) \le \min\{F_s(r)\mathrm{e}^{h(y - r)}, 1\}$, the distribution function of an exponential tail with mean $r - \{1 + \log F_s(r)\}/h$ that lies stochastically below $W + s^{1/2}Z$, so $\mu \ge r - \{1 + \log F_s(r)\}/h$ and $h(r - \mu) \le 1$. Laws in $\B$ satisfy $f_s' \le f_s^2/F_s$, so $(\log f_s)'(r) \le h \le 1/(r - \mu)$; applied to $-W$ this gives $(\log f_s)'(-r) \ge -1/(r + \mu)$. Hence, for $r > M$, $f_s'(r) \le f_s(r)/(r - M)$ and $-f_s'(-r) \le f_s(-r)/(r - M)$. Let $H_s(r) = \pr(|W + s^{1/2}Z| \le r)$. By the heat equation $\partial_sf_s = f_s''/2$,
\[
\partial_sH_s(r) = \tfrac12\{f_s'(r) - f_s'(-r)\} \le \frac{f_s(r) + f_s(-r)}{2(r - M)} = \frac{\partial_rH_s(r)}{2(r - M)}.
\]
The quantile $r(s) = Q_q(|W + s^{1/2}Z|)$ solves $H_s\{r(s)\} = q$ and is continuously differentiable on $s > 0$, with $r'(s) \ge -1/[2\{r(s) - M\}]$ where $r(s) > M$ \citep{steinbrecher2008}. Hence $\{r(s) - M\}_+^2$ has derivative at least $-1$ almost everywhere, and $\{r(\varepsilon) - M\}_+^2 \le \{r(\sigma^2) - M\}_+^2 + \sigma^2 - \varepsilon$. As $\varepsilon \to 0$, $r(\varepsilon) \to Q_q(|W|)$, because a nondegenerate law in $\B$ has a positive density on the interior of its support, so the distribution function of $|W|$ is continuous and strictly increasing where it lies strictly between $0$ and $1$. Since $r(\sigma^2) \le t$, the first inequality follows, and the second from $(d^2 + \sigma^2)^{1/2} \le d + \sigma^2/(2d)$.
\end{proof}

The order $x$ cannot be improved, even for mean-zero laws: if $X$ has density proportional to $\mathrm{e}^{\beta x}$ on $[-\ell, 0]$, $W = X - E(X)$ and $r_0 = Q_q(|W|)$ with $\pm r_0$ interior to the support, then, after scaling $W$ so that the noisy $q$-quantile of $|W + x^{1/2}Z|$ equals $1$, $Q_q(|W|) = 1 + \tfrac12\beta r_0\tanh(\beta r_0)x + o(x)$, because near $\pm r_0$ the density satisfies $f' = \beta f$ and $H_v(r) = H_0(r) + v\{f'(r) - f'(-r)\}/2 + o(v)$ uniformly near $r_0$. For every $q$ the condition holds for small $\beta\ell$, so centring does not replace symmetry.

The order $x^{1/2}$ returns only when the mean is within a bounded number of noise standard deviations of the boundary. With $b_x = 1 - \kappa x^{1/2}$, let
\[
L_q(\kappa) = \sup\{F_Y^{-1}(q) : Y \in \B,\ E(Y) \le -\kappa,\ \pr(Y + Z \le 0) \ge q\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:meanonesided}
Let $\mathrm{e}^{-1} < q < 1$ and $\kappa \ge 0$. Then $L_q(\kappa) = \sup\{v_q(u) : u > 0,\ k_q(u) \ge \kappa\}$; $L_q$ is positive and nonincreasing; for $q > 1/2$, $L_q(\kappa) = c_q$ for $\kappa \le \kappa^*_q$, the largest value of $k_q$ on the compact set of maximizers of $v_q$; and $\kappa L_q(\kappa) \to \{1 - \log(1/q)\}/2$ as $\kappa \to \infty$. In double precision $\kappa^*_q = 6.81$, $20.18$ and $50.07$ at $q = 0.8$, $0.9$ and $0.95$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $a = \log(1/q)$ and $A_q = 1 - a > 0$. For $q > 1/2$, $v_q$ is continuous on $(0, \infty)$, tends to $0$ as $u \to 0$ and to $-\Phi^{-1}(q) < 0$ as $u \to \infty$, and is positive somewhere, so its maximizers form a nonempty compact subset of $(0, \infty)$. If $Y$ is nondegenerate with $q$-quantile $v$, the replacement of fact 5 of \S~\ref{sec:s-prelim}, $Y' = v + a/u - E_u$, lies stochastically below $Y$, so $E(Y') \le E(Y) \le -\kappa$ and $\pr(Y' + Z \le 0) \ge q$, with the same $q$-quantile. For the tail $b - E_u$ the constraints read $b \le b_u(q)$ and $b \le u^{-1} - \kappa$, so the largest quantile is $\min\{v_q(u), A_q/u - \kappa\}$. If $k_q(u) < \kappa$, this minimum is $A_q/u - \kappa$, and since $k_q$ is continuous with $k_q(s) \to \infty$ as $s \to 0$, some $s < u$ has $k_q(s) = \kappa$ and value $A_q/s - \kappa$, larger; so only $u$ with $k_q(u) \ge \kappa$ matter, and there the value is $v_q(u)$. A feasible point mass $y_0$ has $y_0 \le -\kappa \le 0 < L_q(\kappa)$, where positivity follows from~(\ref{eq:smallu}). Since $m_u(b) \ge \Phi(-b)$, $b_u(q) \ge -\Phi^{-1}(q)$, so $k_q$ is bounded on $[u_0, \infty)$ for every $u_0 > 0$, and $k_q(u) \ge \kappa$ forces $u \le \{A_q + o(1)\}/\kappa$ as $\kappa \to \infty$; with $v_q(u) = \{1 + o(1)\}u/2$, by~(\ref{eq:smallu}), this gives $\limsup\kappa L_q(\kappa) \le A_q/2$. Conversely, for large $\kappa$ there is $u_\kappa$ with $k_q(u_\kappa) = \kappa$, by continuity, and $u_\kappa \to 0$; by~(\ref{eq:smallu}), $u_\kappa = \{A_q + o(1)\}/\kappa$ and $L_q(\kappa) \ge v_q(u_\kappa) = \{1 + o(1)\}u_\kappa/2$, so $\liminf\kappa L_q(\kappa) \ge A_q/2$. Monotonicity is clear, and $L_q(\kappa) = c_q$ while a maximizer of $v_q$ is feasible.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:transition}
Let $1 - \mathrm{e}^{-1} < q < 1$, $x > 0$ and $W \in \B$ with $\pr(|W + x^{1/2}Z| \le 1) \ge q$. Then $|E(W)| < 1$, and if $\Delta = Q_q(|W|) - 1 > 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:necessary}
2\{1 - |E(W)|\}\Delta + \Delta^2 \le x.
\end{equation}
Moreover, for every $\kappa \ge 0$, $\lim_{x\to0}x^{-1/2}\{R^{(b_x)}_{q,q}(x) - 1\} = L_q(\kappa)$, and the same limit holds with $\LC$ replaced by $\B$ in the definition of $R^{(b)}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{First part.} Let $V = W + x^{1/2}Z$, with distribution function $F$ in $\B$. By fact 4 of \S~\ref{sec:s-prelim}, $F\{E(V)\} \ge \mathrm{e}^{-1}$ and $1 - F\{E(V)\} \ge \mathrm{e}^{-1}$. If $E(W) \ge 1$, then $\pr(|V| \le 1) \le F\{E(W)\} \le 1 - \mathrm{e}^{-1} < q$, and similarly if $E(W) \le -1$; so $|E(W)| < 1$. Theorem~\ref{thm:mean} with $M = |E(W)|$ and $t = 1$ gives $\Delta \le (d^2 + x)^{1/2} - d$ with $d = 1 - |E(W)|$, which is~(\ref{eq:necessary}).

\emph{Lower bound in the limit.} Take $u$ with $k_q(u) \ge \kappa$ and the law $W = 1 + x^{1/2}\{b_u(q + \eta_x) - E_u\}$ of \S~\ref{sec:s-small}. Its mean is $1 + x^{1/2}\{b_u(q + \eta_x) - u^{-1}\} \le 1 - \kappa x^{1/2}$, because $b_u$ decreases in its argument, and it is positive for small $x$; hence the $\liminf$ is at least $v_q(u)$ for every such $u$.

\emph{Upper bound in the limit.} Suppose that $x_n \to 0$, $h_n = x_n^{1/2}$, and $W_n$ is feasible for $R^{(b_{x_n})}_{q,q}(x_n)$ with $Q_q(|W_n|) - 1 \ge a_0h_n$, where $L_q(\kappa) < a_0 < 1$. By~(\ref{eq:necessary}), $\kappa h_n \le 1 - |E(W_n)| \le \bar\kappa h_n$ with $\bar\kappa = (1 - a_0^2)/(2a_0)$. Replacing $W_n$ by $-W_n$ if necessary, $E(W_n) \ge 0$; let $Y_n = (W_n - 1)/h_n$, so that $-\bar\kappa \le E(Y_n) \le -\kappa$. The law of $T_n = Y_n + Z$ is in $\B$ with a smooth positive density of maximum $M_n$ and distribution function $F_n$, and $F_n(0) \ge q$ while $F_n\{E(T_n)\} \le 1 - \mathrm{e}^{-1}$, so $q - 1 + \mathrm{e}^{-1} \le F_n(0) - F_n\{E(T_n)\} \le M_n\bar\kappa$. For a law in $\B$ with smooth density $g$, maximum $M$ and median $m$, the monotone hazards give $g(m) \ge M/2$ and $\pr(|T - m| > y) \le \mathrm{e}^{-2g(m)y}$, hence $\var(T) \le 2/M^2$. Thus the $\var(Y_n)$ are bounded, the $Y_n$ are tight and uniformly integrable, and along a subsequence $Y_n \to Y$ in distribution with $Y \in \B$, possibly a point mass, $E(Y) \le -\kappa$ and $\pr(Y + Z \le 0) \ge q$. On $\{W_n \ge 0\}$, whose probability tends to one, $(|W_n| - 1)/h_n = Y_n$, so $a_0 \le \{Q_q(|W_n|) - 1\}/h_n \to F_Y^{-1}(q) \le L_q(\kappa)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:meanonesided}, where the quantile converges because a law in $\B$ has a unique $q$-quantile or is a point mass. This contradicts $a_0 > L_q(\kappa)$; if $L_q(\kappa) \ge 1$ there is nothing to prove, because~(\ref{eq:necessary}) gives $\Delta < x^{1/2}$.
\end{proof}

At $q = 0.9$, $L_q$ equals $c_q$ up to $\kappa \approx 20$ and then decays like $1/\kappa$: $L_{0.9}(25) = 0.017393$ and $L_{0.9}(50) = 0.0089448$ (double precision). A mean near the boundary is necessary for a widening of order $x^{1/2}$, not sufficient: a Gaussian law with mean $1.29x^{1/2}$ from the boundary after scaling needs no widening at all.

\subsection{Rules that use known variances}\label{sec:s-kv}

The rules of this subsection use the noise variances $D_i$ and assume Gaussian noise; they are the comparison rules HetLDC, known lower bound and Markov rule of \S~\ref{M-sec:competitors}.

\emph{An order-statistic level.} Let $T = |V|_{(k)}$, let $\bar F$ be the average distribution function of the $|V_i|$, assumed continuous, and let $p_k$ be the $\delta$-quantile of the ${\rm Beta}(k, K + 1 - k)$ distribution.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:beta}
If $k \ge Kp_k + 1$, then $\pr\{\bar F(T) \ge p_k\} \ge 1 - \delta$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\bar F(T) < p_k$ exactly when $N = \#\{i : |V_i| < \bar F^{-1}(p_k)\} \ge k$. $N$ is a sum of independent indicators whose success probabilities average $p_k$, and $k - 1 \ge Kp_k$, so by \citet{hoeffding1956} $\pr(N \le k - 1)$ is smallest when all success probabilities are equal. Hence $\pr(N \ge k) \le \pr\{\Bin(K, p_k) \ge k\} = \pr\{{\rm Beta}(k, K + 1 - k) \le p_k\} = \delta$.
\end{proof}

\emph{HetLDC: the radius for the average kernel.} Let $\bar g(w) = K^{-1}\sum_i\pr(|w + e_i| \le T)$ be the average noisy kernel at $T$. On the event of Proposition~\ref{prop:beta}, $\int\bar g\,dG \ge p_k$. Let $R^{\rm mix}$ be defined by~(\ref{eq:R}), in units in which the threshold is $1$, with the constraint $\int\bar g(Tz)\,dG_T(z) \ge p_k - \omega$ on the law $G_T$ of $W/T$, where $\omega$ bounds the sup-norm error of compressing the $D_i$ to $32$ support points, about $2 \times 10^{-4}$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}, which holds for this kernel, the radius $TR^{\rm mix}$ covers the latent target with probability at least $q$, given the data, on an event of probability at least $1 - \delta$, for every log-concave $G$. This guarantee is for the exact $R^{\rm mix}$; the implementation computes an upper bound on it by a branch and bound over $\mathcal F$ whose closed forms~(\ref{eq:closed}) are evaluated in double precision with a margin of $10^{-9}$, which is not a certificate. Replacing the average kernel by the kernel of a single variance is not safe: with the mean variance the radius is $1.4\%$ too short at mean $x = 0.2$ with lognormal spread $1$ (numerical), and the smallest variance also fails.

The rule with a known lower bound on the variances uses a two-sided version of the constant of Lemma~\ref{lem:onesided}. With $c_{1,q'} = -\infty$, since no law has noisy coverage one at a single end, and $c_{p',1} = \infty$, let
\[
C_{p,q} = \sup_{\alpha_L + \alpha_R \le 1 - p}\ \inf_{\beta_L + \beta_R = 1 - q}\ \max(c_{1 - \alpha_L, 1 - \beta_L},\, c_{1 - \alpha_R, 1 - \beta_R}),
\]
over nonnegative $\alpha$'s and $\beta$'s. Taking $\alpha_L = 0$ shows $C_{p,q} \ge c_{p,q}$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:twosidedC}
For every $x > 0$ and every $W \in \B$ with $\pr(|W + x^{1/2}Z| \le 1) \ge p$, $Q_q(|W|) \le \max\{0, 1 + C_{p,q}x^{1/2}\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\alpha_R = \pr(W + x^{1/2}Z > 1)$ and $\alpha_L = \pr(W + x^{1/2}Z < -1)$, so $\alpha_L + \alpha_R \le 1 - p$, and for $\varepsilon > 0$ take $\beta_L + \beta_R = 1 - q$ with both constants at most $C_{p,q} + \varepsilon$. The law of $Y = (W - 1)/x^{1/2}$ has $\log F_Y$ concave and $\pr(Y + Z \le 0) = 1 - \alpha_R$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:onesided} $F_Y^{-1}(1 - \beta_R) \le C_{p,q} + \varepsilon$ and $\pr\{W > 1 + (C_{p,q} + \varepsilon)x^{1/2}\} \le \beta_R$. The same argument for $-W$ gives $\pr\{W < -1 - (C_{p,q} + \varepsilon)x^{1/2}\} \le \beta_L$, so $\pr\{|W| \le 1 + (C_{p,q} + \varepsilon)x^{1/2}\} \ge q$; let $\varepsilon \to 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:simple}
Let $G \in \B$, let the noise be Gaussian and $k \ge Kp_k + 1$. If $D_i \ge D_{\min}$ for all $i$ and $C_{p',q} < 0$ for some $p'$ with $q \le p' \le p_k$, then $s = \max\{0, T + C_{p',q}D_{\min}^{1/2}\}$ satisfies $\pr_{\mathcal D}\{\pr(|W_{\rm new}| \le s \mid \mathcal D) \ge q\} \ge 1 - \delta$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
On the event of Proposition~\ref{prop:beta} some $i$ has $\pr(|W + e_i| \le T) \ge p_k \ge p'$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:twosidedC} at threshold $T$ and $x = D_i/T^2$ gives $Q_q(|W|) \le \max\{0, T + C_{p',q}D_i^{1/2}\} \le \max\{0, T + C_{p',q}D_{\min}^{1/2}\}$, as $C_{p',q} < 0$.
\end{proof}

At $q = 0.9$, $K = 110$ and $\delta = 0.05$, $p_k = 0.906806$ and ball arithmetic gives $C_{0.9068,0.9} \le -0.114$, hence the known-lower-bound rule $s = T - 0.114D_{\min}^{1/2}$. At $K = 1000$ the order-statistic level of the rank used, $917$, lies below the smallest level with a certified negative constant, $0.9036$, so the rule returns $T$ itself, which is why its width equals that of the Gaussian rank in Table~\ref{M-tab:competitors}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:shapefree}
Let $\bar g$ be a kernel that is symmetric, continuous, strictly decreasing in $|w|$ and tends to $0$ as $|w| \to \infty$, with $m_0 = \bar g(0)$, and suppose that $qm_0 < p < m_0$. Over all laws $G$ on the real line with $\int\bar g\,dG \ge p$, the supremum of $Q_q(|W|)$ is the radius $r$ that solves $\bar g(r) = (p - qm_0)/(1 - q)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\tau = (p - qm_0)/(1 - q) \in (0, m_0)$. Since $m_0 - \bar g(W) \ge 0$, Markov's inequality gives $\pr\{\bar g(W) < \tau\} \le (m_0 - p)/(m_0 - \tau) = 1 - q$, and $\bar g(W) < \tau$ exactly when $|W| > r$, so $Q_q(|W|) \le r$. Conversely, mass $q - \epsilon$ at $0$ and $1 - q + \epsilon$ at the point $w_\epsilon > 0$ with $\bar g(w_\epsilon) = \{p - (q - \epsilon)m_0\}/(1 - q + \epsilon)$ meets the constraint with equality and has $Q_q(|W|) = w_\epsilon \to r$.
\end{proof}

The Markov rule of \S~\ref{M-sec:competitors} is the radius of Proposition~\ref{prop:shapefree} with the average kernel at the level $p_k$ of a rank fixed in advance, the rank for level $0.925$; it is the smallest radius that this single constraint allows without a shape assumption. With $1$ in place of $\bar g(0)$ it contains the bound of \citet{guille2024}.

\section{Certificates in ball arithmetic}\label{sec:s-ball}

Three levels of numerical support are kept apart. Certificates in Arb ball arithmetic \citep{johansson2017}, through \texttt{python-flint}, are proofs: every deciding inequality is evaluated on balls, floating point only proposes points, balls are converted to doubles with outward rounding, and sums of bounds are formed as balls. Upper bounds evaluated in double precision with a margin are labelled numerical. Grid and search values are used only as lower bounds or as proposals.

\emph{Gaussian levels} (\texttt{latentcov.gaussian}). By Lemma~\ref{lem:sampford}, $A$ is decreasing, so bisection on balls brackets $b^*$ by doubles $b_{\rm lo} < b^* < b_{\rm hi}$ with $A(b_{\rm lo}) > \log(1/q) > A(b_{\rm hi})$ proved. The lower end of the enclosure of $\Psi_{\N}(q)$ is $G\{q, m(b_{\rm lo})\}$, a value of the supremand; the upper end is $\Phi(-c) + \phi(c)/m(b)$ at $b = b_{\rm hi}$, which equals $\Psi_{\N}(q_{\rm hi})$ with $q_{\rm hi} = \mathrm{e}^{-A(b_{\rm hi})} > q$, and $\Psi_{\N}$ increases. The enclosures have width below $3 \times 10^{-16}$; at $q = 0.9$, $\Psi_{\N}(0.9) = 0.900803469446498$.

\emph{Symmetric unimodal levels} (\texttt{latentcov.noise\_classes.psi\_su\_enclosure}). The root $r$ of $d$ in \S~\ref{sec:s-su} is bracketed in the same way, using that $d$ decreases, and $g$ is evaluated on balls at both ends of the bracket; the split certificate \texttt{split\_certificate\_su} checks the two-sided level independently.

\emph{Mean-zero log-concave levels} (\texttt{lc\_certificate\_parallel}, \texttt{split\_certificate\_lc}). The branch and bound of \S~\ref{sec:s-lc} evaluates the cut bound on balls, with box ends entering as exact doubles and derived ends such as $1/|k|$ rounded outward; near $k = 0$, $Z_k$ and $\int_0^1v\mathrm{e}^{kv}\,dv$ are evaluated by their series with a remainder bound. The thresholds for the ends of the scale parameter are found by enlarging the parameter until the analytic bound holds on a ball.

\emph{One-sided constants} (\texttt{latentcov.interval.c\_enclosure}). The function $m_u(b)$ decreases in $b$, and in $u$ because $E_u$ is stochastically decreasing in its rate; hence $b_u(p)$ is nonincreasing in $u$ and, on $[u_0, u_1]$, $v_{p,q}(u) \le b_{u_0}(p) - u_1^{-1}\log(1/q)$. A ball with $m_u(b) < p$ proves $b_u(p) < b$. For $u \ge u_{\max}$ the value is at most $b_{u_{\max}}(p)$; for small $u$, with $b(u) = \{\log(1/p) + u^2/2 - \log(1 - u^3)\}/u$, $\mathrm{e}^{-ub + u^2/2} = p(1 - u^3)$ and the Mills ratio gives $\Phi(-b) \le pu^3$ whenever $\phi\{\log(1/p)/u\} \le pu^2\log(1/p)$, which holds on $(0, u_{\min}]$ once it holds at $u_{\min} \le \log(1/p)/2^{1/2}$; so $b_u(p) \le b(u)$ there. The certificates give $c_{0.8} \in [0.0460730, 0.0460731]$, $c_{0.9} \in [0.0190618, 0.0190619]$ and $c_{0.95} \in [0.0084075, 0.0084077]$. For the two-sided constant of Lemma~\ref{lem:twosidedC}: by Lemma~\ref{lem:onesided}, $c_{p',q'} \le \gamma$ if and only if $1 - q' \ge \varphi_\gamma(1 - p')$, with $\varphi_\gamma(\alpha) = \max[0, \sup_u 1 - \exp\{-u(b_u(1 - \alpha) - \gamma)\}]$, the largest latent failure beyond $\gamma$ at one end with noisy failure $\alpha$ there. Hence $C_{p,q} \le \gamma$ if $\varphi_\gamma(\alpha_L) + \varphi_\gamma(\alpha_R) \le 1 - q$ whenever $\alpha_L + \alpha_R = 1 - p$; since $\varphi_\gamma$ is nondecreasing, it suffices to check this at the ends of an adaptive partition of $\alpha_L$, with $\varphi_\gamma$ bounded above by the monotone branch and bound in $u$. This gives $C_{0.9036,0.9} \le -0.057$ and $C_{0.9068,0.9} \le -0.114$.

\emph{The bimodal law} (\texttt{latentcov.interval.bimodal\_blc\_certificate}). The two inequalities of Lemma~\ref{lem:bimodal} are checked on $128$ boxes covering $[a, m_2]$ and $[m_1, b]$, with $a$ and $b$ enclosed by bisection on balls and rounded outward.

\emph{Double precision only.} The transition values $\kappa^*_q$ and $L_q(\kappa)$, the grid maxima of \S~\ref{sec:s-feasible} and the upper bounds of $R^{\rm mix}$ used by HetLDC are computed in double precision and are not certified.

\section{Numerical studies: design and full results}\label{sec:s-numerical}

Throughout, $\delta = 0.05$, latent reliability is the probability over calibration samples that the latent coverage given the data is at least $q$, and latent coverage given the data is computed from the exact latent distribution function. Ranks: the marginal rank is $\lceil q(K + 1)\rceil$; the usual rank is the smallest $k$ with $\pr\{\Bin(K, q) \le k - 1\} \ge 1 - \delta$; the class ranks use the levels of Table~\ref{M-tab:map}.

\subsection{Mixed noise laws}\label{sec:s-mixed}

The latent law is the extremal exponential tail of \S~\ref{sec:s-reliability} in the small-noise limit, where the left end does not matter. In units of the tail scale, a unit whose noise is $c\epsilon$, with $\epsilon$ of unit variance, has noisy coverage $h(\epsilon, c) = E\{\min(1, q\mathrm{e}^{-c\epsilon})\}$ at the latent $q$-quantile, by Proposition~\ref{M-prop:general}. Each unit draws its noise law from a mixture within one class and its scale $c = c_0L$, with $L = 1$ (equal scales) or $L$ lognormal with log-scale $0.7$ and mean $1$ (heterogeneous scales), independently. The $|V_i|$ are then independent and identically distributed, and the latent reliability of rank $k$ is $\pr\{\Bin(K, H) \le k - 1\}$ exactly, with $H = E\,h(\epsilon, c_0L)$ computed by quadrature and Gauss--Hermite integration over $L$. The common scale $c_0$ is chosen adversarially, to maximize $H$. The mixtures are: within the symmetric unimodal class, uniform noise with weight $1$, $1/2$ or $1/4$ and the rest split equally between Gaussian, Laplace and $t_3$ noise, at $q = 0.8$ and $0.9$; within the mean-zero log-concave class, the centred exponential law $E_1 - 1$ with weight $1$, $1/2$ or $1/4$ and the rest split between Gaussian, uniform, Laplace and the reflected law $1 - E_1$, and the two signs with weight $1/2$ each, at $q = 0.9$. \subsection{Comparison with existing methods}\label{sec:s-competitors}

\emph{Design.} The latent laws are those of \S~\ref{sec:s-sameinfo}, the variances are drawn as there, $q = 0.9$, and there are $300$ data sets at $K = 110$ and $200$ at $K = 1000$. The noise is Gaussian, Laplace, or drawn per unit from \{Gaussian, uniform, Laplace, $t_3$\}, with the same variances. Every rule outputs a latent interval whose coverage given the data is exact. A new unit has no variance of its own, so the rules that need one draw it from the law that generated the $D_i$, with $400$ draws per data set, and coverage and width are averaged over the draws.

\emph{Rules.} (i) The usual rank and the ranks of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank} for Gaussian and symmetric unimodal noise, and the rank at level $(1 + q)/2$ without a shape assumption; none uses the variances. (ii) LatentCP \citep*{zheng2026}, single level: the noisy conformal threshold $t_\gamma$ at level $1 - \gamma$ with $\gamma = \alpha/2$, $\alpha = 0.1$, inverted through the Gaussian forward model with the new unit's variance $d$ to $\{w : \{1 - p_\gamma(w, d)\}/\gamma \le 1/\alpha\}$, with $p_\gamma(w, d) = \pr(|w + d^{1/2}Z| \le t_\gamma)$; tuned versions with one or two levels, the levels and weights chosen on a random half of the data by mean width and calibrated on the other half (for $K = 1000$ the grid is thinned to $12$ levels and the width criterion uses $100$ tuning variances, for computing time); and a version with the threshold at a high-probability rank, our adaptation. (iii) The Markov rule of Proposition~\ref{prop:shapefree} with the known variances. (iv) A Fay--Herriot interval: an intercept-only Fay--Herriot model fitted by restricted maximum likelihood with the $D_i$ known, and a tolerance multiplier from a parametric bootstrap with $300$ replicates, the bootstrap $(1 - \delta)$-quantile of the multiplier each refit needs to cover $N(\hat\mu, \hat A)$ with probability $0.9$, adapted from \citet*{chatterjee2008}. (v) HetLDC, the radius of \S~\ref{sec:s-kv}, run at $K = 110$ under Gaussian noise only, because its branch and bound takes about $2.5$ minutes of one core per data set. (vi) The known-lower-bound rule of Corollary~\ref{cor:simple}. (vii) The deconvolution threshold of \citet*{cohen2025}, Algorithm~1, in our implementation: a histogram of the noisy scores with bin width $h$, a deconvolution by nonnegative least squares with the average Gaussian kernel and ridge penalty $0.01$, and the threshold lowered in steps of $0.01$ from the noisy conformal threshold while the estimated latent coverage stays at least $0.9$; with $h = 0.01$ empty bins are masked, as in their algorithm, and with $h = 0.2$ they are fitted as zeros. The tuning grids of LatentCP and the bin widths and scan step of the deconvolution threshold are our choices; the original papers do not fix them for this setting.

\subsection{Latent laws that differ between units}\label{sec:s-hetero-sim}

Calibration unit $i$ has the latent law $W_i = b_i - E_1/u_i$, an exponential tail with rate $u_i$ and $Q_q(|W_i|) = r_i = 1 + \tau_i/u_i$, so that $\tau_i$ measures the distance from the new unit's quantile $r_0 = 1$ in units of the tail scale, and Gaussian noise $\sigma_iZ$ with $\sigma_i$ chosen adversarially, to maximize $\pi_i = \pr(|V_i| < r_0)$, which has a closed form. The latent reliability of rank $k$ is $\pr(\sum_i X_i \le k - 1)$ with independent $X_i \sim$ Bernoulli$(\pi_i)$. When the unit parameters are drawn independently from a design, the $X_i$ are independent and identically distributed with mean $H = E\pi_i$, exact for the homogeneous and point-mass designs and a Monte Carlo mean over $4000$ draws, with standard error about $2 \times 10^{-4}$, for the heterogeneous ones. The designs are: homogeneous extremal, $\tau_i = 0$ and $u_i = 200$; all units slightly easier than the new one, $r_i = 0.999995$, that is $\tau_i = -10^{-3}$ with $u_i = 200$ or $\tau_i = -10^{-2}$ with $u_i = 2000$; heterogeneous with the condition of Corollary~\ref{M-cor:hetero}, $u_i = 200L_i$ with $L_i$ lognormal with log-scale $0.7$ and $\tau_i = |N(0, 0.02^2)|$; the same with $10\%$ of units easier, $\tau_i = -0.02$; and the new unit a random unit of the two-type population of point masses of \S~\ref{sec:s-hetero}, with $\sigma = 10^{-4}$.

Table~\ref{tab:s-hetero} shows three things. The bound of Corollary~\ref{M-cor:hetero} is attained by homogeneous extremal laws, and heterogeneity among units that satisfy the condition only adds slack. The condition has no slack at the extremal law: lowering every $r_i$ by a factor $1 - 5 \times 10^{-6}$ lowers the reliability of the rank of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank} to $0.094$ at $K = 10^6$ and $q = 0.9$; whether a violation is small is relative to the latent tail scale, and with a tail ten times steeper the same violation gives $0.085$ at $K = 10^4$. And when the new unit is a random unit of a heterogeneous population, the bi-log-concave ranks fail while the shape-free rank keeps its guarantee.

With one draw of the parameters of $K$ units held fixed, the units are independent but not identically distributed, and the reliability is a Poisson-binomial probability, computed exactly by recursion. When the condition holds, $\max_i\pi_i$ equals $\Psi_{\N}(q)$ to five digits, attained by the units with $\tau_i$ near $0$, and the reliability of the rank of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank} is $0.9989$ ($K = 10^3$) and $1.0000$ ($K = 10^4$) at $q = 0.9$, and $0.9955$ and $1.0000$ at $q = 0.8$. With $10\%$ easier units, $\max_i\pi_i$ exceeds $\Psi_{\N}(q)$, $0.9187$ at $q = 0.9$, but the reliability stays at least $0.991$, as the general form of \S~\ref{sec:s-hetero} allows.

\subsection{Contaminated latent laws}\label{sec:s-contam-sim}

Each unit has the latent law $(1 - \xi)G + \xi H$, with $G$ the extremal law of \S~\ref{sec:s-reliability} for the target $q_0 = q/(1 - \xi)$, an exponential tail with rate $u/s$ and end just beyond the threshold, $H$ the point mass at $1 + s\eta'$, and Gaussian noise $N(0, s^2)$, with $s = 10^{-3}$ and $\eta' = 10^{-6}$. Then $\pr(|W| \le 1) = q$, so $r = 1$, and the noisy coverage $H_\xi = \pr(|V_i| < 1)$ has a closed form; it is within $2 \times 10^{-8}$ below $p_\xi(q)$. The latent reliability of rank $k$ is $\pr\{\Bin(K, H_\xi) \le k - 1\}$, exactly.

\section{The school-district application}\label{sec:s-app}

\emph{Data.} The Academic Performance Index population \texttt{apipop} is taken from the CRAN package \texttt{survey}, version 4.5, checked against a SHA-256 digest. It has one record per California school; districts with at least five schools, $325$ of them, form the population. Covariates are the socio-demographic variables (shares of students on subsidised meals, English language learners and new to the school; parents' education in five categories and on average; shares of fully and emergency credentialed teachers; enrolment; response rate), a year-round indicator and the school type; the second set adds the 1999 index. Missing covariates are replaced by their medians.

\emph{Design.} Each replication splits the districts at random into $110$ training, $110$ calibration and $105$ evaluation districts and samples $n = 2$ or $3$ schools per training and calibration district without replacement. A ridge regression of the 2000 index on the covariates, with the penalty chosen by cross-validation over $30$ values, is fitted to the sampled schools of the training districts. For a calibration district $c$ the score $V_c$ is the mean residual of its sampled schools and $\hat D_c = (1 - n/N_c)s_c^2/n$, with $s_c^2$ the sample variance of those residuals and $N_c$ the number of schools in the district, estimates its design variance with $n - 1$ degrees of freedom. The target of an evaluation district is the mean residual over all its schools, its true mean minus the population mean of the predictor, and its interval is centred at zero on this scale except for the Fay--Herriot interval, centred at the fitted mean. Coverage is the share of the $105$ evaluation districts covered, a finite-population coverage. There are four conditions, the two covariate sets with $n = 2$ and $3$, and $120$ replications each.

\emph{Rules.} The ranks of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank} for the three bi-log-concave rows of Table~\ref{M-tab:map} are all $105$ at $K = 110$, equal to the usual rank, and use no variance estimate; the rank without a shape assumption is $109$. The Fay--Herriot interval, LatentCP and the Markov rule plug in the $\hat D_c$ as if known, a case that no guarantee covers. LatentCP needs the variance of the new district, which is unknown; in this application variant it is drawn from the calibration estimates, $200$ draws per replication.

The guarantee of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:rank} still needs the noise of the direct estimates to lie in the stated class, independent of the latent residuals, and the calibration districts to be no easier than a new one in the sense of Corollary~\ref{M-cor:hetero}; neither can be confirmed from these data. With two or three schools, the direct estimates are means of very few residuals, so their noise need not be close to Gaussian, which is one reason to state the guarantee for the symmetric unimodal or log-concave class; at $K = 110$ the rank is the same.
