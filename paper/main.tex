\begin{quotation}
\noindent {\it Abstract:}
Prediction intervals for a latent quantity, such as the true mean of an unsampled small area, are often calibrated on noisy proxies. We ask what latent coverage such calibration guarantees when the noise law and variance are unknown, differ between units and are independent of the latent residuals. Under bi-log-concavity of the latent law we give the sharp noisy level that guarantees a latent coverage target, exactly for Gaussian and symmetric unimodal noise; unimodality alone gains nothing. The resulting conformal rank needs no noise variances, keeps a high-probability guarantee for every noise configuration in the class, and was 12 to 48 percent narrower in simulations than the guarantee without a shape assumption. The usual threshold falls short of the level by less than one thousandth under Gaussian noise at a target of ninety percent. When a share of the latent law departs from bi-log-concavity, the exact level grows with that share; we recommend calibrating at a small positive share. Simulations and a school-district illustration accompany the theory.

\vspace{9pt}
\noindent {\it Key words and phrases:}
Bi-log-concavity, conformal prediction, measurement error, small area estimation, tolerance interval.
\par
\end{quotation}\par

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Suppose that a residual $W$, the latent target minus a centre fitted on independent training data, is observed only through $V = W + e$, with noise $e$ independent of $W$. A survey statistician who calibrates a prediction interval for the true mean of an unsampled area on the direct estimates of sampled areas is in this position \citep{fay1979, rao2015}, as is a conformal analyst whose calibration responses carry measurement error \citep{einbinder2024, cohen2025}. The threshold $t$ at which the noisy scores $|V|$ have coverage $p$ is observable; the quantity of interest is the latent coverage $\pr(|W| \le t)$. The noise law and its variance are often unknown, or known only through rough estimates, and they differ between units: direct estimates of areas with different sample sizes have different variances.

The interval is for a unit without its own noisy observation, such as an unsampled area, predicted synthetically; intervals for sampled areas that combine a direct estimate with a model are a different problem. The practice is to calibrate on the noisy scores as if they were latent, with the usual conformal rank. We ask when this covers the latent target, and what is the smallest correction that makes it do so when the noise laws and scales are unknown and differ between units.

Two answers are known. If $W$ is symmetric and unimodal about zero, no interval of length $2t$ carries more of its mass than $[-t, t]$, so $\pr(|W + e| \le t) \le \pr(|W| \le t)$ for every independent noise and the noisy threshold is conservative; this is the one-dimensional case of the theorem of \citet{anderson1955}, and \citet[Proposition~1]{einbinder2024} prove a version for conformal intervals. Without a shape assumption, median-zero noise turns a noisy level $1 - \alpha$ into a latent level $1 - 2\alpha$ \citep[Proposition~2.3]{guille2024}. Symmetry is a strong assumption, since residuals of area means of skewed outcomes are asymmetric, and the shape-free answer is very costly.

We take the latent law to be bi-log-concave \citep*{dumbgen2017}: its distribution function $F$ is continuous and both $\log F$ and $\log(1 - F)$ are concave. The class contains the log-concave laws and some multimodal ones and does not require symmetry. The results determine how close noisy calibration is to valid without symmetry, what exact validity requires, and how fast the guarantee weakens when the shape assumption fails.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{A sharp transfer.} Unimodality of the latent law, even with mean zero, gives nothing over no shape assumption (Proposition~\ref{prop:latentshape}). Under bi-log-concavity we give the noisy level $\Psi_{\E}(q)$ that guarantees latent coverage $q$, exactly for Gaussian and symmetric unimodal noise (Theorems~\ref{thm:classes} and~\ref{thm:gauss}). For Gaussian noise at $q = 0.9$ it exceeds $q$ by $8.0 \times 10^{-4}$: noisy coverage $0.9$ guarantees latent coverage $0.89918$ and no more.
\item \emph{The price of a doubtful shape assumption.} If a share $\xi$ of the latent law is arbitrary, the exact level under Gaussian or symmetric unimodal noise is $(1 - \xi)\Psi_{\E}\{q/(1 - \xi)\} + \xi/2$ for $q \ge 0.53$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:contam}). At $q = 0.9$ under Gaussian noise it is $0.90081$, $0.90322$, $0.90564$ and $0.92517$ for $\xi = 0$, $0.5\%$, $1\%$ and $5\%$. Each percent of the latent law outside the class raises the level by about $0.005$; as the exact guarantee of the uncontaminated level tolerates almost no such share in large samples, we recommend calibrating at a small positive $\xi$.
\item \emph{A variance-free rank, and when the usual one suffices.} Calibrating at these levels gives a conformal rank that keeps the latent guarantee with probability at least $1 - \delta$ for every noise law and scale in the class, possibly different between units (Theorem~\ref{thm:rank}); in the worst case no smaller rank is valid, and at $K = 10^3$ the raise is one or two ranks for Gaussian and symmetric unimodal noise without contamination, and $24$ ranks at $\xi = 5\%$. Against the shape-free guarantee, the only other one with the same information, the interval was $12$ to $48\%$ narrower in simulations (\S~\ref{sec:cost}). The usual rank loses its guarantee when the residual density rises toward an end of the interval, as when a biased centre puts the mode beyond the threshold (Propositions~\ref{prop:fail} and~\ref{prop:centred}, \S~\ref{sec:need}).
\end{enumerate}

Related methods target different quantities or use more information (Table~\ref{tab:methods}). LatentCP \citep*{zheng2026} gives a marginal latent guarantee through a known forward model; \citet*{cohen2025} and deconvolution with known heteroscedastic or unknown symmetric errors \citep{delaigle2008, delaigle2016} estimate the latent law or threshold without a coverage guarantee; \citet*{ignatiadis2022} bound functionals of a nonparametric latent law with high probability when the noise law is known and common to all units, and \citet*{soloff2025} estimate it under heteroscedastic Gaussian noise. Bounds for noisy labels, contamination or distribution shift \citep*{sesia2025, penso2025, clarkson2024, barber2023, cauchois2024, feldman2026} control marginal coverage up to a term that does not shrink with the sample, whereas here the high-probability guarantee is at stake. Our rank is the tolerance limit of \citet{wilks1941}, the training-conditional rank of \citet{vovk2012} and \citet{bian2023}, with a raised level, and \citet[Appendix~C.3]{guille2024} already carry their shape-free bound to a high-probability guarantee; what is new is the sharp level under a shape assumption, its price when the assumption fails, and when the raise is needed. Conformal intervals for small areas, survey or hierarchical data cover observable responses \citep{bersson2024, wieczorek2023, dunn2023}; \citet{zhang2026} cover the latent expected values of sampled units, given the sample, the closest target to ours but for areas with their own data. Intervals for latent small-area means \citep{yu2018, burris2020, diao2014, yoshimori2014, hallmaiti2006, chatterjee2008, armstrong2022} use sampling variances, known or estimated with their uncertainty taken into account \citep{wangfuller2003, you2006, dass2012}, and a model for the latent effects; tolerance intervals for true values \citep{wang1994} and for random-effects models with heterogeneous error variances \citep{liao2005, lin2008} rely on normality. The rank studied here needs neither variance estimates nor a parametric latent model.

\section{Setting}\label{sec:setting}

Let $\LC$ be the class of log-concave laws on the real line, including point masses, and $\B \supset \LC$ the class of laws that are point masses or have a continuous distribution function $F$ with $\log F$ and $\log(1 - F)$ concave. Both classes are closed under translation and scaling, and convolution with a log-concave law preserves them (Supplementary \S~\ref{S-sec:s-prelim}); $\B$ contains some bimodal mixtures of normal laws (Supplementary Lemma~\ref{S-lem:bimodal}).

A class $\E$ of noise laws is closed under scaling and negation. The classes considered are the Gaussian laws $\N = \{\sigma Z : \sigma > 0\}$; symmetric unimodal laws; mean-zero log-concave laws; mean-zero unimodal laws; and, for rules without a latent shape assumption, median-zero laws. For a noise law and a latent law, the noisy coverage of $[-t, t]$ is $\pr(|W + e| \le t)$ and the latent coverage is $\pr(|W| \le t)$.

In calibration, conditionally on the training data, $W_1, \ldots, W_K, W_{\rm new}$ are independent latent residuals and $e_i = \sigma_i\epsilon_i$ are independent noises, independent of the residuals, with laws of $\epsilon_i$ in $\E$ that may differ between units and fixed unknown scales $\sigma_i > 0$. Let $\mathcal D$ denote the calibration data and $T = |V|_{(k)}$, the $k$th smallest of $|V_1|, \ldots, |V_K|$. The interval $[-T, T]$ is placed around the fitted centre of a new unit. We call
\[
\pr_{\mathcal D}\{\pr(|W_{\rm new}| \le T \mid \mathcal D) \ge q\}
\]
the latent reliability of rank $k$: the probability, over calibration samples, that the interval attains latent coverage $q$. A rank is valid at level $1 - \delta$ if its latent reliability is at least $1 - \delta$ for every latent law and noise configuration in the model. The usual rank is the smallest $k$ with $\pr\{\Bin(K, q) \le k - 1\} \ge 1 - \delta$ \citep{wilks1941, vovk2012}, valid when the scores are noise free. $Q_q$ denotes the lower $q$-quantile, $\Phi$ and $\phi$ the standard normal distribution function and density. Noisy levels are written $\Psi_{\E}$ for one end of the interval, $p_{\E}$ for both ends, $p^\zeta_{\E}$ for centred residuals, $p_\xi$ for a contaminated latent law and $p_{\xi,\eta}$ when also the noise is contaminated.

\section{Coverage transfer}\label{sec:transfer}

\subsection{Unimodality does not suffice}

Let $\beta_{\E} = \inf_{\epsilon \in \E}\pr(\epsilon \ge 0)$: $1/2$ for Gaussian, symmetric unimodal and median-zero noise, $\exp(-1)$ for mean-zero log-concave noise, attained by the centred exponential law, and $0$ for mean-zero unimodal noise.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:latentshape}
Let $\beta_{\E} > 0$ and $1 - \beta_{\E} < p < 1$. Then $\pr(|W| \le 1) \ge 1 - (1 - p)/\beta_{\E}$ for every $W$ and every noise with law in $\E$ and $\pr(|W + e| \le 1) \ge p$. If the infimum defining $\beta_{\E}$ is approached by laws continuous at $0$, then for every $\gamma > 0$ there are a unimodal $W$ with $E(W) = 0$ and such a noise with $\pr(|W| \le 1) \le 1 - (1 - p)/\beta_{\E} + \gamma$. If $\beta_{\E} = 0$, approached in the same way, then for every $p < 1$ and $\gamma > 0$ there are such $W$ and noise with $\pr(|W| \le 1) \le \gamma$.
\end{proposition}

The lower bound is that of \citet[Proposition~2.3]{guille2024}; Proposition~\ref{prop:latentshape} adds that unimodal latent laws with mean zero attain it. With median-zero noise, noisy coverage $0.9$ thus guarantees only latent coverage $0.8$. The extremal laws place a thin layer of mass just outside the threshold, and the noise carries a fraction $1 - \beta_{\E}$ of it inside. Bi-log-concavity excludes such a layer: concavity of $\log F$ makes $f/F$ nonincreasing, so the density cannot jump up at the threshold.

\subsection{One end of the interval}

For a class $\E$ let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psi}
\Psi_{\E}(q) = \sup_{\epsilon \in \E} E\{\min(1, q \exp(-\epsilon))\}.
\end{equation}
The extremal latent laws are exponential tails, because concavity of $\log F_Y$ limits how fast mass can accumulate below a quantile: with $X_u$ exponential with rate $u > 0$, the law of $b - X_u$ has distribution function $\min\{1, \exp(u(y - b))\}$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:general}
Let $\epsilon$ have a law in $\E$ and be independent of $Y$, with $\log F_Y$ concave. If $\pr(Y + \epsilon \le 0) \ge p > \Psi_{\E}(q)$, then $F_Y(0) \ge q$. If $p < \Psi_{\E}(q)$, some exponential tail $Y$ with $F_Y(0) < q$ and some $\epsilon$ with law in $\E$ have $\pr(Y + \epsilon \le 0) > p$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that $v = F_Y^{-1}(q) > 0$, let $a = \log(1/q)$ and let $u$ be a supergradient of $\log F_Y$ at $v$. Then $F_Y(y) \le \min\{1, q\exp(u(y - v))\}$, so the exponential tail $v + a/u - X_u$ lies stochastically below $Y$. Adding $\epsilon$ preserves this, and as $v > 0$ and $u\epsilon$ has a law in $\E$,
\[
p \le \pr(Y + \epsilon \le 0) \le \pr(a/u - X_u + \epsilon \le 0) = E\{\min(1, q \exp(-u\epsilon))\} \le \Psi_{\E}(q).
\]
This uses $F_Y(v) = q$. Since a concave function is continuous on the interior of its domain, the only other case is an atom of mass at least $q$ at the left end $v$ of the support of $Y$; then $Y + \epsilon \le 0$ requires $\epsilon < 0$, and $p \le \pr(\epsilon < 0) \le \lim_{u\to\infty} E\{\min(1, q \exp(-u\epsilon))\} \le \Psi_{\E}(q)$. Conversely, $a - X_1$ with noise $\epsilon$ attains $E\{\min(1, q \exp(-\epsilon))\}$; shift it slightly to the right.
\end{proof}

Applied to $Y = (W - t)/s$ for any scale $s > 0$, Proposition~\ref{prop:general} controls the right end of $[-t, t]$; applied to $-W$, it controls the left end. For a one-sided interval $(-\infty, t]$ it is the complete answer under concavity of $\log F_W$ alone.

\subsection{Both ends}

A noisy failure probability $1 - p$ may be split in any way between the two ends, and each end transfers separately. Let $\lambda_{\E}(p) = \sup\{q' \in (0, 1) : \Psi_{\E}(q') < p\}$, with $\sup\emptyset = 0$, and $\vartheta_{\E}(a) = 1 - \lambda_{\E}(1 - a)$, the largest latent failure probability at one end compatible with noisy failure probability $a$ there; $\vartheta_{\E}(0) = 0$ when $\beta_{\E} > 0$. Define the sufficient noisy level
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pE}
p_{\E}(q) = \inf\Big\{p : \sup_{a_L, a_R \ge 0,\ a_L + a_R \le 1 - p}\{\vartheta_{\E}(a_L) + \vartheta_{\E}(a_R)\} \le 1 - q\Big\}.
\end{equation}
Noisy coverage above $p_{\E}(q)$ at threshold $t$ gives latent coverage $q$ for every $W \in \B$ and every noise with law in $\E$ (Supplementary Lemma~\ref{S-lem:twoend}). Putting all the failure at one end shows that $\Psi_{\E}(q) \le p_{\E}(q)$, and if $\Psi_{\E}$ is convex the worst split puts the whole failure at one end, so that $p_{\E}(q) = \Psi_{\E}(q)$ (Supplementary Lemma~\ref{S-lem:convex}).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:classes}
Let $q > \exp(-1)$.
\par\noindent (i) For symmetric unimodal noise, $\Psi_{\E}(q) = \{1 + q \exp(-\varrho)\}/2$, where $\varrho > 0$ solves $(1 + \varrho)\exp(-\varrho) = (1 + \log q)/q$, attained by uniform noise; $\Psi_{\E}$ is strictly convex, so $p_{\E}(q) = \Psi_{\E}(q)$.
\par\noindent (ii) For mean-zero unimodal noise, $\Psi_{\E}(q) = 1$.
\end{theorem}
Part (i) holds because symmetric unimodal laws are scale mixtures of uniform laws (Supplementary Proposition~\ref{S-prop:su-exact}). For part (ii), a noise spread uniformly over $[-a, 0]$, balanced to mean zero by a small mass far above zero, makes $E\{\min(1, q\exp(-\epsilon))\}$ arbitrarily close to $1$.

\begin{proposition}[Computer-assisted]\label{prop:lc}
With mean-zero log-concave noise, $\Psi_{\E}(q)$ is the supremum over centred log-affine laws on segments. At $q = 0.9$ it lies in $(0.90517, 0.9052)$, the lower end attained by the centred exponential law, and certified splits of the failure probability give $p_{\E}(0.9) \le 0.906$.
\end{proposition}
The reduction follows from the localization theorem of \citet{fradelizi2004}; the bounds come from a branch and bound in ball arithmetic, and are certified only at $q = 0.9$ (Supplementary \S\S~\ref{S-sec:s-lc} and~\ref{S-sec:s-ball}).

\subsection{Gaussian noise}

For the Gaussian class $\N$ the supremum in (\ref{eq:psi}) is over the scale only. With $a = \log(1/q)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G}
\Psi_{\N}(q) = \sup_{s > 0} \Gamma(q, s), \qquad \Gamma(q, s) = \Phi(-a/s) + q\exp(s^2/2)\Phi(a/s - s).
\end{equation}
Let $m(b) = \phi(b)/\Phi(b)$ and $A(b) = m(b)\{b + m(b)\} = 1 - \var(Z \mid Z \le b)$, which decreases strictly from $1$ to $0$ as $b$ runs over the real line \citep{sampford1953}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:gauss}
Consider the Gaussian class $\N$.
\par\noindent (i) If $q \le \exp(-1)$, then $\Psi_{\N}(q) = 1/2$, approached as $s \to \infty$ and not attained.
\par\noindent (ii) If $q > \exp(-1)$, then $\Gamma(q, \cdot)$ has a unique maximiser $s^* = m(b^*)$, where $b^*$ is the unique solution of $A(b^*) = \log(1/q)$, and
\[
\Psi_{\N}(q) = \Phi(-c) + \phi(c)/m(b^*), \qquad c = b^* + m(b^*).
\]
\par\noindent (iii) $\Psi_{\N}$ is continuously differentiable, with $\Psi_{\N}'(q) = \exp(m(b^*)^2/2)\,\Phi(b^*)$ for $q > \exp(-1)$, and strictly convex on $(\exp(-1), 1)$. Hence $p_{\N}(q) = \Psi_{\N}(q)$ for every $q \in (\exp(-1), 1)$.
\end{theorem}

The proof, in Supplementary \S~\ref{S-sec:s-gauss-exact}, reduces the first-order condition to $s = m(b)$, a Mills ratio of the noise, and the envelope derivative to $\exp(m(b)^2/2)\Phi(b)$, whose monotonicity is that of the truncated variance $\var(Z \mid Z \le b)$.

At $q = 0.8$, $0.9$ and $0.95$, $\Psi_{\N}(q) = 0.8044106$, $0.9008035$ and $0.9501590$ to seven decimals; noisy coverage $0.9$ guarantees latent coverage $0.89918$ and no more. For Gaussian and symmetric unimodal noise the transfer is nontrivial exactly when $q > \exp(-1)$; for mean-zero log-concave noise $\Psi_{\E}(q) \ge 1 - \exp(-1)$ for every $q$, from scaled centred exponential noise. \section{Calibration with unknown, heterogeneous noise}\label{sec:procedure}

\subsection{A valid rank}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:rank}
In the setting of \S~\ref{sec:setting}, let the latent residuals have a common law $G \in \B$. Then
\[
\pr_{\mathcal D}\{\pr(|W_{\rm new}| \le T \mid \mathcal D) \ge q\} \ge \pr\{\Bin(K, p_{\E}(q)) \le k - 1\}.
\]
Conversely, if $\pr\{\Bin(K, \Psi_{\E}(q)) \le k - 1\} < 1 - \delta$, there are a log-concave $G$ and a noise law in $\E$, the same in every unit, for which the latent reliability is below $1 - \delta$. Hence the smallest rank valid for every $G \in \B$ lies between the smallest $k$ with $\pr\{\Bin(K, \ell) \le k - 1\} \ge 1 - \delta$ at $\ell = \Psi_{\E}(q)$ and at $\ell = p_{\E}(q)$, which coincide for Gaussian and symmetric unimodal noise.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $r = Q_q(|W|)$ for $W \sim G$ and $N = \#\{i : |V_i| < r\}$. Then
\[
\pr(|W_{\rm new}| \le T \mid \mathcal D) \ge q \iff T \ge r \iff N \le k - 1.
\]
For $t < r$, $\pr(|W| \le t) < q$, so by the transfer of \S~\ref{sec:transfer} at threshold $t$, $\pr(|V_i| \le t) \le p$ for every $p > p_{\E}(q)$, whatever the law and scale of $e_i$; letting $t \uparrow r$, $\pr(|V_i| < r) \le p_{\E}(q)$. $N$ is a sum of independent indicators with these success probabilities, so it is stochastically smaller than $\Bin\{K, p_{\E}(q)\}$. Conversely, for $p < \Psi_{\E}(q)$ the one-ended laws of \S~\ref{sec:transfer} give a log-concave $G$ and a noise with $\pr(|V_i| < r) > p$, so the latent reliability is at most $\pr\{\Bin(K, p) \le k - 1\}$, which tends to $\pr\{\Bin(K, \Psi_{\E}(q)) \le k - 1\}$ as $p \uparrow \Psi_{\E}(q)$.
\end{proof}

The bound holds unit by unit, so the noise laws and scales need not be known or shared; it uses independence of the units, not only exchangeability. For Gaussian and symmetric unimodal noise the rank is sharp: along the laws of the converse, the latent reliability of the smallest valid rank tends to exactly $1 - \delta$ as $K \to \infty$. These laws are degenerate, an exponential tail whose scale tends to zero relative to the threshold, with noise of the same order, so sharpness is an infimum that is approached but not attained; with a known lower bound on the noise relative to the threshold the needed level can only be lower, a question taken up for Gaussian noise in Supplementary \S~\ref{S-sec:s-known}. The procedure needs only $K$, $q$, $\delta$ and a noise class: take the level from Table~\ref{tab:map}, whose levels are rounded up and so are sufficient, or from the accompanying software, which returns the upper end of a certified enclosure; take $k$ as the smallest rank with $\pr\{\Bin(K, \text{level}) \le k - 1\} \ge 1 - \delta$; and report $[\hat\mu_{\rm new} - T, \hat\mu_{\rm new} + T]$ with $T = |V|_{(k)}$. A level rounded to the nearest value may be too low and lower the rank by one.
With known positive scales $s_i$, fixed before calibration, the normalized scores $|V_i|/s_i$ and the interval $[-s_{\rm new}T, s_{\rm new}T]$ have the same guarantee if the normalized residuals $W_i/s_i$ and $W_{\rm new}/s_{\rm new}$ have a common law in $\B$, or more generally under the ordering of Corollary~\ref{cor:hetero} for the normalized residuals, because both classes are closed under scaling; bi-log-concavity of each $W_i/s_i$ alone is not enough, since scales that do not match the residuals rescale the target. At $K = 110$ the three bi-log-concave rows of Table~\ref{tab:map} give the usual rank, $k = 105$, against $109$ and $110$ without a shape assumption.

Table~\ref{tab:map} summarizes the choice of level. The class levels cost one to six ranks at $K = 10^3$, the levels without a latent shape assumption about fifty. Skewed or discrete noise, as for means of a few units of a skewed population, calls for the log-concave level and, if even log-concavity of the noise is doubtful, for a level without a shape assumption on the noise (\S~\ref{sec:need}).


\subsection{When the usual rank is valid}\label{sec:usual}

The rank of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} exceeds the usual rank because the level $\Psi_{\E}(q)$ exceeds $q$ by a fixed amount, while consecutive ranks differ in level by about $1/K$. At the extremal laws this raise cannot be dropped.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fail}
Let $q \in (0,1)$. There are a log-concave law $G$ and $D > 0$ such that, with $e_i \sim N(0, D)$ for all $i$, $\pr_{\mathcal D}\{\pr(|W_{\rm new}| \le |V|_{(k_K)} \mid \mathcal D) \ge q\} \to 0$ as $K \to \infty$ for every sequence of ranks with $k_K/K \to q$, such as the marginal and, for fixed $\delta$, the usual high-probability rank.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:gauss}, $\Psi_{\N}(q) > q$ for every $q \in (0, 1)$. Take $p$ with $q < p < \Psi_{\N}(q)$. The converse construction in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} gives a log-concave latent law and $D > 0$ with $H = \pr(|V_i| < r) > p$, $r = Q_q(|W|)$; the latent reliability of rank $k$ is $\pr\{\Bin(K, H) \le k - 1\}$, which tends to zero because $k_K/K \to q < H$.
\end{proof}

The latent coverage itself barely moves: at the extremal Gaussian-noise law, the usual rank's latent coverage tends to $0.89918$ at $q = 0.9$. What fails is the guarantee, once $K$ is of order $q(1 - q)/\{\Psi_{\N}(q) - q\}^2$: the reliability falls below $0.5$ at $K \approx 2.2 \times 10^4$ and $3.8 \times 10^5$ for $q = 0.8$ and $0.9$ (\S~\ref{sec:need}). The extremal law, however, is far from a typical residual. It is an exponential tail $W = t + s\{\log(1/q) - X_1\}$ with $s$ small: almost all of its mass lies just inside the threshold, on one side, so that its mean is close to $t$. A residual with a correctly fitted centre does not look like this.

How far from typical the extremal law must be is measured by a dial on centring. For $\zeta \ge 0$ let
\[
p^\zeta_{\E}(q) = \sup\{\pr(|W + e| < 1) : W \in \LC,\ |E(W)| \le \zeta,\ \pr(|W| < 1) \le q\},
\]
the supremum being also over noise laws in $\E$ and their scales. With the threshold scaled to $1$, $\zeta$ bounds the bias of the fitted centre relative to the half-width of the interval; the extremal law above has $\zeta$ close to $1$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:centred}
Let $\E$ be the Gaussian or the symmetric unimodal class, $\exp(-1) < q < 1$ and $\zeta \ge 0$.
\par\noindent (i) If the latent residuals have a common log-concave law with $|E(W)| \le \zeta\,Q_q(|W|)$, the bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} holds with $p^\zeta_{\E}(q)$ in place of $p_{\E}(q)$, for noise laws and scales in the class that differ between units.
\par\noindent (ii) $p^\zeta_{\E}(q) = \Psi_{\E}(q)$ for $\zeta \ge 1$, and $q < p^\zeta_{\E}(q) \le \Psi_{\E}(q)$ for every $\zeta \ge 0$.
\par\noindent (iii) In the supremum, $W$ may be restricted to point masses and to laws with density proportional to $\exp(-\psi)$ on a segment, with $\psi$ the maximum of at most three affine functions, and the noise to Gaussian or to uniform laws.
\end{proposition}

Part (i) is the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank}, applied at $r = Q_q(|W|)$ itself. In part (ii), the extremal law above has its mean inside the interval, and a slightly tilted centred law whose density rises through the right end gains coverage from small noise, at the order of the noise variance; so the usual rank is not exactly valid even for centred residuals. Part (iii) follows from the localization theorem of \citet{fradelizi2006}, with the coverage and the two bounds on the mean as linear constraints, and reduces $p^\zeta_{\E}(q)$ to a search over six parameters (Supplementary \S~\ref{S-sec:s-centred}). Table~\ref{tab:centred} gives the values. They are numerical: each is attained by an explicit law, checked in $30$-digit arithmetic, so no lower level is valid; that it is the supremum rests on a global search, which no other log-concave law among $3700$ tried exceeded.

\subsection{Robustness of the latent assumptions}\label{sec:robust}

The common latent law in Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} is the assumption a user is least able to check. Two relaxations keep an exact guarantee.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:hetero}
In the setting of \S~\ref{sec:setting}, let $W_1, \ldots, W_K, W_{\rm new}$ be independent with laws $G_1, \ldots, G_K, G_0$, and let $r_0 = Q_q(|W_{\rm new}|)$. If $G_i \in \B$ and $Q_q(|W_i|) \ge r_0$ for every $i = 1, \ldots, K$, then the bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} holds. The law $G_0$ need not be bi-log-concave.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first display in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} uses only the law of $W_{\rm new}$, with $r_0$ in place of $r$. For $t < r_0 \le Q_q(|W_i|)$, $\pr(|W_i| \le t) < q$, and the transfer applied to $G_i \in \B$ and the noise of unit $i$ gives $\pr(|V_i| < r_0) \le p_{\E}(q)$.
\end{proof}

The condition says that the new unit is no harder to cover than any calibration unit at the $q$-quantile of $|W|$. It is sufficient, not necessary: a few easier calibration units are absorbed by slack in the others (Supplementary \S~\ref{S-sec:s-hetero}). It cannot simply be dropped: at the extremal law lowering every $Q_q(|W_i|)$ by a factor $1 - 5 \times 10^{-6}$ lowers the reliability to $0.094$ at $K = 10^6$, and if the new unit is a random draw from a population of units with different laws, a shape assumption on the components gives nothing, because mixtures of point masses are arbitrary laws. If the noise scale is random and the latent residual depends on it, as is common when estimation precision predicts the parameter \citep{chen2026}, the corollary applies conditionally on the scale; without that, a large noise scale exactly for units whose residual lies just outside the threshold carries them inside, as can happen when the noise variance is a function of the latent quantity, such as $\theta_i(1 - \theta_i)/n_i$ for estimated proportions.

The second relaxation lets a fraction of the latent law be arbitrary. For $0 \le \xi < 1$ let $\B_\xi = \{(1 - \xi)G + \xi H : G \in \B,\ H \text{ any law}\}$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:contam}
Let $\E$ be the Gaussian or the symmetric unimodal class, $q \ge 0.53$ and $0 \le \xi < 1 - q$, and let
\[
p_\xi(q) = (1 - \xi)\,\Psi_{\E}\{q/(1 - \xi)\} + \xi/2,
\]
and $p_\xi(q) = (1 + q)/2$ for $\xi \ge 1 - q$. Noisy coverage above $p_\xi(q)$ at a threshold guarantees latent coverage $q$ at that threshold for every $W \in \B_\xi$ and every noise with law in $\E$, and no lower level does, even with $G$ log-concave and $H$ a point mass. The level increases continuously from $\Psi_{\E}(q)$ at $\xi = 0$ to the shape-free level $(1 + q)/2$ at $\xi = 1 - q$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} and Corollary~\ref{cor:hetero} hold with $\B_\xi$ and $p_\xi(q)$ in place of $\B$ and $p_{\E}(q)$, the rank again being sharp.
\end{proposition}

The bound $q \ge 0.53$ is what the proof needs and covers every $q$ of practical interest. The worst contamination is a layer just outside the threshold, half of which the noise carries inside, as in Proposition~\ref{prop:latentshape}; the rest of the latent law is then extremal for the reduced target $q/(1 - \xi)$. The proof is in Supplementary \S~\ref{S-sec:s-contam}. At $q = 0.9$ under Gaussian noise the level rises by about $0.48\,\xi$: it is $0.90322$, $0.90564$ and $0.92517$ for $\xi = 0.5\%$, $1\%$ and $5\%$, rounded up, and the ranks at $K = 10^3$ are $919$, $922$ and $940$, against $917$ for $\xi = 0$ and $962$ without a shape assumption. Conversely, a given rank $k$ is valid over $\B_\xi$ exactly when $p_\xi(q)$ is at most the $\delta$-quantile of the Beta$(k, K + 1 - k)$ law, which gives the contamination it tolerates: the rank $105$ at $K = 110$ tolerates $\xi = 1.2\%$, but the rank of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} at $K = 10^3$ tolerates only $0.09\%$, and at $K = 10^4$ only $0.01\%$. Because the rank of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} tolerates almost no contamination at large $K$, we recommend calibrating at $p_\xi(q)$ with a small positive $\xi$, such as $1\%$, which costs five ranks at $K = 10^3$. If calibration and new units are independent draws from a population whose latent law is in $\B_\xi$, the guarantee holds for a random new unit.

Noise outside the class can be priced in the same way, and sharply. If the noise of each unit is drawn, independently of its latent residual, from a law in $\E$ with probability $1 - \eta$ and from an arbitrary law otherwise, the noisy coverage of the second part is at most one, so noisy coverage above $p_{\xi,\eta}(q) = (1 - \eta)p_\xi(q) + \eta$ guarantees latent coverage $q$ for every $W \in \B_\xi$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} holds at this level. At $q = 0.9$ under Gaussian noise with $\xi = 0$, $\eta = 1\%$ and $5\%$ give $0.90180$ and $0.90577$, and ranks $918$ and $922$ at $K = 10^3$. The level is attained: with the extremal latent law and an arbitrary noise component equal to $-r$, which moves every such unit inside the interval, the noisy coverage tends to $p_{\xi,\eta}(q)$. Arbitrary noise includes a deterministic bias, so $\eta$ is the share of units whose noisy score may be biased; a percent of it costs about $0.001$ in level, against about $0.005$ for a percent of the latent law.

\section{Numerical studies}\label{sec:numerical}

All reliabilities below are latent, with $\delta = 0.05$. Where a law is specified exactly, the reliability is computed exactly from a binomial distribution; otherwise latent coverage given each data set is exact and the reliability is a share of data sets.

\subsection{When the correction is needed}\label{sec:need}

At the extremal law for Gaussian noise, scaled so that the noisy coverage at the latent $q$-quantile of $|W|$ equals $\Psi_{\N}(q)$, Figure~\ref{fig:reliability} compares the marginal rank, the usual rank and the rank of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank}; Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:s-reliability} gives the exact values. At $q = 0.9$ the usual rank has reliability $0.918$ at $K = 10^4$ and $0.150$ at $K = 10^6$, against $0.952$ and $0.950$ for the rank of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank}. The loss is in the guarantee, not in the coverage: at $K = 10^6$ the mean latent coverage of the usual rank's interval is $0.89968$, and that of the rank of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} is $0.90050$. A reliability of $0.150$ means that $15\%$ of calibration samples give an interval with latent coverage at least $0.9$, not a coverage of $15\%$.

The usual rank loses the guarantee as $K$ grows, while the rank of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} stays above $0.95$ and tends to it. Noise laws drawn per unit within a class delay the failure of the usual rank but do not remove it; the class rank kept reliability at least $0.9501$ in all $20$ such cases (Supplementary \S~\ref{S-sec:s-mixed}). Under contamination of the extremal law the rank of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} fails as well, with reliability $0.51$ at $K = 10^4$ for $\xi = 1\%$ and $q = 0.9$, while the rank at level $p_\xi(q)$ keeps at least $0.950$ (Supplementary \S~\ref{S-sec:s-contam-sim}).

Between the extremal and the benign laws, the reliability was computed exactly for $8028$ designs (Supplementary \S~\ref{S-sec:s-intermediate}): log-concave residuals that are symmetric, smoothly skewed or J-shaped, with the mode at an end of the support, biased up to $\zeta = 0.75$; Gaussian or uniform noise with variance $0.001$ to $3$ times the latent one, equal or heterogeneous; small $K$; and estimated proportions, whose noise variance depends on the latent value. For the symmetric and smoothly skewed residuals of the study the usual rank was valid at every $K$ and noise level. It lost reliability only for J-shaped residuals under small noise: for a reflected exponential with $\zeta = 0.5$ and noise variance $0.01$ at $q = 0.8$, $0.934$, $0.841$ and $0.319$ at $K = 10^3$, $10^4$ and $10^5$, against $0.967$, $0.982$ and $0.999$ for the rank of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank}, which kept at least $0.950$ in every design. These are findings for the designs: to first order in the noise variance $v$, the excess $H - q$ is $\tfrac12 v\{f'(r) - f'(-r)\}$, so a smooth density gains coverage from small noise when $f'(r) > f'(-r)$ and loses it when $f'(r) < f'(-r)$; a density that increases outward at one end is necessary for a gain but not sufficient, since a steeper increase at the other end can outweigh it. The expansion is for a fixed law that is twice differentiable near $\pm r$ and noise of finite variance as $v \to 0$, and it is not uniform: a law with an edge within a few noise standard deviations of the threshold can gain coverage although $f'(r) < f'(-r)$, and noise of infinite variance, which the symmetric unimodal class allows, is not covered.

A rank computed for one noise class can fail under noise outside it (Supplementary \S~\ref{S-sec:s-misspec-sim}). At $q = 0.9$ the Gaussian rank survives Laplace, logistic and $t_5$ noise at the extremal law, but not uniform or skewed noise: under centred exponential noise its reliability is $0.58$ at $K = 10^4$. Strongly skewed noise outside the log-concave class, such as the sampling error of a mean of a few units of a skewed population, exceeds even the log-concave level; the noise class must be chosen with the skewness of the noise in mind.

\section{Application: school districts}\label{sec:application}

The California Academic Performance Index data \citep{lumley2004} give the 2000 index of every school; districts with at least five schools form the population. Each of $480$ replications, in four conditions, splits the districts into $110$ training, $110$ calibration and the remaining evaluation districts, samples $n = 2$ or $3$ schools per training and calibration district, fits a ridge predictor and scores each calibration district by its sample-mean residual; the target of an evaluation district is its true mean residual. The design variance of a score, estimated from two or three schools, has one or two degrees of freedom, which is the situation the variance-free rank is made for.

The comparison that matters is with the rule that uses the same information without a shape assumption (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:s-app-levels}). At $q = 0.9$ the bi-log-concave ranks are $105$, equal to the usual rank, against $109$ without a shape assumption, and their intervals were $22$--$26\%$ shorter; both covered at least $90\%$ of the evaluation districts in all $480$ replications, a share whose Wilson lower limit is $0.969$ per condition. At $q = 0.95$ the rule without a shape assumption has no valid rank at $K = 110$, while the bi-log-concave rank $109$ gives a finite interval that covered at least $95\%$ in every replication. Calibrating for $5\%$ contamination raises the rank at $q = 0.9$ to $107$ and the width by $6$--$13\%$, still shorter than without a shape assumption. Rules that plug in the noisy variance estimates behaved as their information suggests: the Fay--Herriot tolerance interval was $16$--$21\%$ shorter than the class rank but covered fewer than $90\%$ of the districts in up to $9.2\%$ of the replications of a condition, and LatentCP was $4.6$--$5.7\%$ wider. The assumptions cannot be confirmed here: the districts form a finite population, the latent law is a mixture over districts, and the noise of a mean of two or three schools is discrete (Supplementary \S~\ref{S-sec:s-app}).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

Without symmetry, noisy calibration is close to valid but not exactly so: under bi-log-concavity and Gaussian noise at $q = 0.9$ the sharp transfer puts the needed level less than a thousandth above $q$, and $0.0044$ above at $q = 0.8$; a share $\xi$ of the latent law outside the class raises it by about $0.48\,\xi$ at $q = 0.9$, and the usual threshold falls short mainly for residual densities that rise toward an end of the interval. The paper is about validity. Calibrating on noisy scores makes the interval much wider than the oracle radius, $25$--$86\%$ in \S~\ref{sec:cost} with noise variance about half the latent one, and only knowledge of the noise variances can recover part of that width (Supplementary \S~\ref{S-sec:s-known}); the correction studied here adds at most a few percent. At the worst centred log-concave laws found numerically the loss of reliability is negligible at any realistic calibration size; the exact correction, a raise of the level from $q$ to $\Psi_{\E}(q)$, needs no noise variances, costs at most a few ranks and keeps a high-probability guarantee for every noise configuration in the class. The ordering of Corollary~\ref{cor:hetero} and the share $\xi$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:contam} are the assumptions that matter most and are hardest to check. Bi-log-concavity cannot be checked on the latent scale, but convolution with log-concave noise preserves it: with a common log-concave noise law, which the heterogeneous setting does not provide, a confidence band for the signed noisy residuals that contains no bi-log-concave distribution function \citep*{dumbgen2017} rules it out; the bands of \citet*{laha2021} could serve the same purpose. A known Gaussian noise variance allows the threshold to be widened instead of its rank raised, by an amount of the order of the noise standard deviation, or of its variance when the latent mean is away from the threshold (Supplementary \S~\ref{S-sec:s-known}).

The centred levels of Proposition~\ref{prop:centred} are numerical below $\zeta = 1$ and are for log-concave residuals only; a certificate, and an extension to $\B$, would complete them. Two further questions remain open. For mean-zero log-concave noise the two-sided level is known only within $(0.90517, 0.906]$ at $q = 0.9$, and the extremality of the centred exponential law is supported by certified computation but not proved. Whether bi-log-concavity is necessary for a nontrivial transfer, or some weaker condition excluding a layer of mass at the threshold suffices, is also open.
