# Intermediate designs: exact reliability between the extremal and the benign

Status (2026-10-14): numerical study, exact up to quadrature (no Monte Carlo in the reported
numbers). Code: `src/latentcov/intermediate.py`, `experiments/r10_intermediate.py` (`make
intermediate`), `tests/test_intermediate.py`. Numbers: `results/intermediate*.csv` (the full `intermediate.csv` and the cache
`intermediate_H.csv` are regenerated by `make intermediate`, not tracked). In the manuscript:
Sec. 5.1 paragraph and Supplement S7 (Table S-intermediate).

Purpose: the referees found the numerical designs either extremal (built to break the usual rank)
or benign (every rule has reliability >= 0.998). This study fills the gap with designs a user could
meet: miscentred skewed residuals of a misspecified mean model, a range of noise-to-signal ratios,
heterogeneous noise variances, uniform noise, small K, and estimated proportions whose noise
variance depends on the latent value.

## Method

Units are i.i.d. As in the proof of Theorem 3, with r = Q_q(|W|) and N = #{i : |V_i| < r},
latent coverage of [-T, T] is at least q iff N <= k - 1, and N ~ Bin(K, H) with
H = pr(|V| < r). So the latent reliability of rank k is pr{Bin(K, H) <= k - 1} exactly, and
everything reduces to computing H. If H <= q the usual rank is valid at every K; if H > q its
reliability tends to 0 as K grows, from K of order q(1 - q)/(H - q)^2. Theorem 3 says
H <= Psi_E(q) whenever its assumptions hold.

H is computed as q + D, D = int f(w) {k(w) - 1(|w| < r)} dw, with k(w) = pr(|w + e| < r) the
noise kernel (Gaussian: two normal cdfs; uniform: piecewise linear). The integrand vanishes away
from +-r for small noise, so the excess H - q has no cancellation. scipy quad on pieces split at
+-r, +-r +- multiples of the noise scale, and the kinks of the latent density (support ends, the
Laplace mode); tolerance 1e-16 absolute. r solves pr(|W| < r) = q by Brent's method from both
tails of the cdf. Heterogeneous variances sigma^2 = rho L / E L, log L ~ N(0, 0.7^2):
Gaussian noise by 96-point Gauss-Hermite in log L (D is analytic in sigma); uniform noise by
adaptive quad in log L split where a breakpoint +-r +- a of the kernel meets a latent kink or the
other end (a = r), since there D(a) has kinks that leave Gauss-Hermite with errors ~1e-6.

Checks (`results/intermediate_check.csv`, `check_diff` in `intermediate_H.csv`):
* 11 homogeneous designs against mpmath at 30 digits (independent pdf and kernel code, direct
  coverage integrals rather than the deficit form): |diff| <= 3e-17; latent coverage at r equals
  q within 2.4e-16.
* every heterogeneous Gaussian design: Gauss-Hermite against adaptive quad in log L, |diff| <=
  3.3e-16; every heterogeneous uniform design against Simpson with 4001 points, |diff| <= 1.3e-10
  (Simpson's own error); three against 20001-point Simpson, |diff| <= 6e-14.
* proportions: 4 designs against mpmath (|diff| <= 2.5e-16) and Monte Carlo with 4e7 draws
  (within 2.5 standard errors; the 2.5 one rechecked with 4e8 draws: 0.4 standard errors).
* the sup over the noise scale of H - q, for every latent law and bias, lies below the centred
  level p^zeta_E(q) - q of R08 (`results/centred_levels.csv`) at matching zeta, by at least
  1.1e-7; at q = 0.8 the reflected exponential nearly attains it (below).

Ranks: rank_for_level vectorized over K (binomial quantile, rank_for_level itself within 1e-9 of
a tie; tested equal). The minimum over K uses all K in [29, 3000] and about 9000 log-spaced K up
to 1e6. "First K below 0.95" uses rel < 0.95 - 1e-12.

## Designs (8028 design x q combinations, q = 0.8, 0.9, 0.95, delta = 0.05)

1. Latent W = W0 + b, W0 standardized (mean 0, variance 1), all log-concave (so in B):
   * skewed smooth: centred gamma(2), gamma(5), skew-normal(alpha = 4), and their reflections;
   * J-shaped (mode at a support end, i.e. a hard edge with the density largest there): centred
     exponential, centred half-normal (an addition to the brief: a hard edge with a flat density),
     and their reflections;
   * symmetric references: normal, Laplace.
   Bias b >= 0 solves b / Q_q(|W0 + b|) = zeta, zeta in {0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5} and 0.75
   (extension); the reflections give the other sign of the bias. zeta = 1 is not attainable for
   these laws (zeta(b) < 1 for all b).
2. Noise sigma eps, eps Gaussian or uniform (unit variance), rho = sigma^2 / var(W) in
   {0.1, 0.3, 0.58, 1, 3} and the extension {0.001, 0.003, 0.01, 0.03}; homogeneous
   (sigma^2 = rho) or heterogeneous (sigma^2 = rho L / E L, L lognormal with log-scale 0.7).
   The extension was added after a first scan showed that H - q is never positive for rho >= 0.1
   (below); the "spec" grid in the csv is zeta <= 0.5 and rho >= 0.1.
3. Rules: usual rank (level q); rank of Theorem 3 (Gaussian noise: Psi_N(q); uniform noise: the
   symmetric unimodal level Psi_SU(q), sufficient end of the certified enclosure).
4. K in {30, 60, 110, 1000, 1e4, 1e5} in `intermediate.csv`; minimum over K in [29, 1e6].
5. Proportions: theta ~ Beta(a, b), (a, b) in {(2, 18), (1, 9), (3, 12), (1.5, 6), (2, 3),
   (5, 5)} (means 0.1-0.5, all log-concave since a, b >= 1) and Beta(0.5, 4.5) (mean 0.1, not
   log-concave and not even bi-log-concave: its hazard is infinite at 0 and first decreases);
   W = theta - c, c = the mean, or the mean -+ half a standard deviation (zeta 0.19-0.45);
   V = W + sqrt(theta(1 - theta)/n) Z, n in {20, 50, 200} and 1000 (extension). The noise is not
   independent of W, so Theorem 3 does not apply. `H_indep_minus_q`: the same latent law with
   independent homogeneous Gaussian noise of the same mean variance, for comparison.

Envelope (`intermediate_envelope.csv`): sup of H - q over 61 log-spaced rho in [1e-5, 3]
(and over 41 n in [10, 1e6] for proportions), for every latent law, zeta, q, noise and scale.

## Findings

What the numbers show (all exact up to quadrature error <= 1e-10):

**(a) The requested grid (zeta <= 0.5, rho >= 0.1) is benign for every latent law.** H < q in
all 3600 such independent-noise design x q combinations (largest H - q = -2.5e-3), so the usual
rank is valid at every K; its smallest reliability over K in [29, 1e6] is 0.958, Theorem 3's
0.959. With noise
variance at least a tenth of the residual variance, the noise spreads the noisy scores so much
more than it can move mass inward that the noisy threshold is conservative, skewed or not.

**(b) Smooth skewed and symmetric laws never give H > q, at any noise level.** For gamma(2),
gamma(5), skew-normal(4) (both orientations), normal and Laplace, and every zeta <= 0.75, the
sup over rho in [1e-5, 3] of H - q is negative, approached as rho -> 0 (it is -O(rho)). This is
the small-noise expansion of notes/centred.md Step 5(b): H - q = (v/2){f'(r) - f'(-r)} + O(v^2),
and for a unimodal law whose mode lies inside (-r, r), f'(r) <= 0 <= f'(-r). Miscentring up to
zeta = 0.75 does not push the mode of these laws past the threshold.

**(c) The gap is filled by J-shaped residuals with small noise.** H > q needs the density to rise
through one end of the interval, i.e. a mode at or beyond the threshold. The exponential and
half-normal with their edge on the side the bias points to (the "reflected" laws for b > 0) do
this. sup over rho of H - q (homogeneous noise, x 1e-4):

of about 1.4-2 (the large-variance units are conservative). At q = 0.8 the exponential nearly attains the centred level

0.95 it reaches the level only for zeta >= 0.75. For centred residuals (zeta = 0) only q = 0.8
gives H > q (the edge of the centred exponential sits just outside -r there: r = 0.95).

Worst cases by q over all independent-noise designs: minimum usual reliability at K <= 1e5 is
1.0e-4 (q = 0.8), 0.59 (q = 0.9), 0.86 (q = 0.95); at K <= 1e4 it is 0.48, 0.88, 0.93. With
the bias within the requested range (zeta <= 0.5) the q = 0.8 minimum at K <= 1e5 is 0.06, and
at K = 1e4 0.74. The first K with reliability below 0.90 is 484 at the worst design and
1358-7099 for the zeta = 0.5, rho = 0.01, q = 0.8 exponential designs. "First K below 0.95" is
at most 130 for 70% of the designs with H > q (up to 22070 for the smallest excesses), but it
reflects the sawtooth of the integer rank: the reliability there is 0.9458-0.9500.

**(e) Theorem 3 never fails.** In every independent-noise design H < Psi_E(q), by at least
2.9e-5 (refl. exponential, zeta = 0.75, q = 0.95, uniform noise); its reliability is >= 0.9502
at every K in [29, 1e6]. This is the theorem (all latent laws are log-concave), but the margin
shows how close ordinary laws get: within 3e-5 of the level at q = 0.95.

**(f) Small K.** At K = 30 and 60 the two rules coincide except q = 0.8, K = 30, uniform noise
(28 against 29). At q = 0.9, K = 30 both use k = 30 = K; at q = 0.95, K = 30 no rank is valid
for either (1 - 0.95^30 < 0.95; Theorem 3 at q = 0.9 needs K >= 29). The only small-K shortfall
is 0.947 at K = 30 (q = 0.8, uniform, refl. exponential, zeta = 0.75, rho = 0.03), against
0.987 for Theorem 3's rank 29. Below K of a few hundred the usual rank's own slack absorbs excesses
of 1e-3: at H = q its reliability is 0.956, 0.969, 0.968 at K = 30, 60, 110 (q = 0.8) and 0.958,
0.986, 0.969 (q = 0.9), against 0.957 and 0.952 at K = 1e3 and 1e4.

**(g) Proportions: benign, and the dependence helps.** For all 252 combinations H < q (largest
H - q = -2.6e-4, at n = 1000) and H < Psi_N(q) by >= 4.2e-4; the usual rank's reliability is
>= 0.951 at every K in [29, 1e6], also for the non-log-concave Beta(0.5, 4.5). Over n in
[10, 1e6] the sup of H - q is still negative (-2.6e-7, as n -> inf). So the violated
independence assumption does no harm here. The comparison with independent noise of the same
mean variance shows why: Beta(1, 9) and Beta(0.5, 4.5) are J-shaped at theta = 0, and with
c = mean + sd/2 their edge sits just outside -r; independent noise then gives H - q = +1.3e-3
and +6.0e-3 at n = 1000, q = 0.8 (for Beta(0.5, 4.5), outside the class, even H > Psi_N(q) by
1.6e-3), whereas the binomial variance theta(1 - theta)/n vanishes at the edge and gives
H - q = -3.8e-3 and -5.0e-3. Beta(a, b) is log-concave iff a, b >= 1.

## Reading

* The usual rank fails in practice only for J-shaped residuals (a hard edge with the density
  largest there, e.g. a floor or ceiling of the outcome on the side the fitted centre is biased
  towards), small noise (variance <= 3% of the residual variance), and q = 0.8 or a bias of a
  third or more of the half-width. There the loss is real at moderate K: reliability 0.84 at
  K = 1e4 and 0.32 at 1e5 for a bias of half the half-width at q = 0.8.
* Typical skewed residuals (gamma, skew-normal) with any bias up to 0.75 of the half-width, and
  any noise-to-signal ratio, never make the usual rank invalid; the noise is conservative for
  them. So the "benign" verdict of the earlier designs is not an artefact of choosing benign
  laws: it holds for every smooth unimodal law with the mode inside the interval.
* Theorem 3's rank holds everywhere, including in the proportion designs that violate its
  independence assumption, at the cost of one to a few ranks (e.g. 826 against 822 at K = 1000,
  q = 0.8).

## Open points

* The designs are i.i.d. units; heterogeneous noise variances enter only through the mixture over
  L (the exact reliability is binomial with the averaged H). Fixed heterogeneous units would need
  the Poisson-binomial recursion of R06.
* Proportions with noise variance increasing in |W| near the threshold (rather than
  theta(1 - theta)/n, which is smooth and vanishes at the edge) were not tried; the paper's
  remark on dependent noise concerns that case.
* Mean-zero log-concave noise is not included (its level is certified only at q = 0.9).
